# Blind review notes (review2)

Judged from the images alone. 16 of 20 answers are high confidence, 4 are medium. Every prompt was used once and the final mapping is a clean one-to-one assignment. No image needed more than one tie-break (see r03/r08 and r13/r20).

## Per image

**r01 -> p02-spiked-lizard (high)**
Armoured polygon-cell hide, a row of cream spikes down the back, and a ball-with-spikes club on the tail. The club reads, but the tail is a thin neck leading to a ball, not a "thick, club-like tail". In the head close-up the skin facets look like a hard-edged mosaic. The body is a huge barrel on short legs with a small head, and a dark sphere sits under the chest in the front view.

**r02 -> p01-wolf (medium)**
Grey body, paler belly, pointed snout, ears and a digitigrade stance. These are the only wolf cues. There is no fur: the skin is smooth with camo blotches. The tail is a short tapering fin, not a bushy tail. The mouth is a row of serrated croc/shark teeth. The snout is not especially long. The legs are uniform stilt tubes that merge into one column in the front view, and the body is very narrow (29 cm wide for 1.8 m). It reads as a grey lizard-dog. Colour is what separates it from the black hound (r10).

**r03 -> p15-gecko (medium)**
Flat, wide body low to the ground, legs splayed out sideways, five long toes per foot. The head is a smooth blob with eyes on top and no visible mouth. The toes are thin and spidery, not "big toes" or adhesive pads. It could just as well be a salamander or a sprawl lizard. I assigned it to the gecko only because r08 fits the "tiny, very long tail, spotted" prompt better. The tail is moderate, not very long.

**r04 -> p19-rhino (high)**
Big nose horn with a smaller horn right behind it, grey cell-scale hide, heavy legs. Clear. Faults: the eyes are tiny and set at the very front of the head, the tail is a tiny spike, and there are two extra ear cones that look like more horns. The rear 3/4 view shows lumpy fused blobs under the hindquarters. The cell pattern is faceted and shows hard shading artefacts at close range.

**r05 -> p04-green-serpent (high)**
Green with regular yellow bands and two large fangs. The fangs are oversized, straight and walrus-like, and hang outside the mouth. The body is a perfectly straight rod in every view, so the 6.6 m "giant" reads as a toy. In the side and 3/4 views it is only a few pixels tall. The head is a flat slab with a visible cracked-mosaic skin.

**r06 -> p17-goblin (high)**
Small, upright, green, with a big round head and big yellow eyes. Unambiguous. Faults: the head is a sphere with no neck, no mouth or nose, and the spikes (ears) point backward like horns. The body is a slug-like pillow with thin tube arms and legs. The hunched stance looks like a leaning pillar. The mottled green is soft and blotchy.

**r07 -> p03-horn-beetle (high)**
Black glossy body, orange spots, one big curved horn, six thin legs. Very recognisable. Faults: the body is a smooth bean or seed with no elytra split, head or thorax segmentation. The legs are thin tubes. The horn is a cone growing from a tucked head. The orange eyes blend into the orange spots.

**r08 -> p07-sprawl-lizard (medium)**
Tiny (51 cm), four sprawled legs, a very long thin tail. The body is sandy and plain, so the "spotted back" is nearly invisible. A few faint dark dashes sit on the tail and a large brown blotch sits on the head, which is the wrong place. The head panel shows a pinched, lumpy mouth. The front view looks like a spider with legs stuck straight out. It matches the prompt mainly by proportions.

**r09 -> p11-sea-serpent (high)**
Dark blue back with a pale belly, a row of spikes along the whole back, a big toothy head. The blue/white split is excellent. Faults: the spikes are tiny and thin, with just one big spike at the nape in the front view. There is nothing aquatic about it: no fins, no flattened tail, no crest. It reads as a rigid eel. The teeth are oversized and evenly spaced. The creature is a few pixels in the 3/4, side and rear panels.

**r10 -> p20-nightmare-hound (high)**

## Most important problems across all images (most severe first)

1. **Faces and heads are the weakest part everywhere.** Heads are blobs: no real mouth, nose or muzzle, tiny or cartoon-sphere eyes, zigzag teeth lines. Head close-ups often show a creature-less crop (r01, r04, r13, r14, r16). Features that define the prompt (troll tusks, goblin face, demon head, cobra hood, wolf snout) are missing or misplaced.
2. **Missing defining silhouette features.** The cobra has no hood (r19). The wolf has no fur and no bushy tail (r02). The troll's "tusks" are brow horns (r15). The ant's mandibles are tiny hooks and it has no antennae (r11). The scorpion has no pincers (r16). The sea serpent has no aquatic features and tiny spikes (r09). The spiked lizard's club is a ball on a thin neck (r01). The long-neck's legs read as an ostrich (r12).
3. **One surface look for everything.** The same soft blotchy camo noise is used on the wolf, gecko, goblin, troll, boar, bear, scorpion and lizards, so fur, skin and scale are not distinguishable. There is no fur, hair or feather anywhere. Scale and cell patterns on the rhino, lizard and snakes show hard facets and a cracked-mosaic look, while the raptor head is flat-shaded low-poly.
4. **Limbs and bodies are uniform tubes.** Legs have no thickness change at joints, no hands or feet, and merge into a single column in the front and rear views (r02, r10, r17, r18). The bipeds (r06, r14, r15) are leaning pillars with thin legs and no hands. Rear views show lumpy fused blobs (r04, r13, r20).
5. **Snakes are rigid straight rods** (r05, r09, r19) with no curvature or coil, and they occupy a few pixels in the 3/4, side and rear panels, so the sheet tells you almost nothing about them. The cobra stands at a hard 90 degree elbow.
6. **Pattern weak or misplaced.** r08's "spotted back" is nearly invisible and the dark blotch lands on the head. The nightmare hound's "grime" is barely visible. The gecko has thin long toes instead of big toes.
7. **Camera framing.** Head close-ups clip the creature or show only a blob. Long, thin creatures are tiny in the 3/4 and rear panels. The header gives length for quadrupeds but height/width/depth for bipeds, which makes scale hard to compare.
