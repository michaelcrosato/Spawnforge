# Blind review notes, gate 12 suite B

All twenty renders matched one prompt each; no two renders competed for the same prompt after
the clear ones were taken. Notes are ordered by render number.

**r01 -> b10-centaur (confidence: high).** A horse-like barrel body on four legs with a small
black hoof at each foot, an upright torso with two arms, and two swept-back horns on the head.
It is the only creature with an upright torso on a quadruped body. Closest rival: none really
(b12 boar for the horns/hooves, but the torso settles it). What is wrong: the torso is a pale,
featureless slab with no chest, shoulders or waist, and it joins the horse body with a dark
collar rather than a believable withers. The head is a long reptilian snout with a smile, not a
human or even humanoid face, and the ear is a goat-like flap at the side. The arms are thin,
hang forward in odd bent poses, and end in flat paddle hands with no readable fingers (the top
view shows them reaching forward like a mantis). The legs are spindly sticks under a long
sausage barrel (2.4 m long, with almost no chest depth, no rib cage, no rump), and the hooves are
tiny dark dots like boots, not real hooves. The tail is a stippled brush that reads as noisy
fur. Overall it looks like a lizard-headed goat person on a stilt-legged log, not a centaur.

**r02 -> b19-armoured-burrower (confidence: high).** Banded armour over the back and tail, an
armadillo-like head shield, a pointed snout and big black digging claws on the forepaws. Closest
rival: b11 sea turtle (shell), dismissed because it has no flippers and does have bands and
claws. What is wrong: the armour is only partly banded. The front dome over the shoulders is a
smooth plate and the bands begin only mid-back, so it reads as a shell with a banded rear. The
head shield looks like a pot lid set on top of the skull with crossed ribs, and two small
horn-like points stick out of it. The snout is long and thin, more anteater or mole than
armadillo. The forelegs are thin and the claws long, black and bear-like, while the hind legs
are almost hidden and the hind claws are not clearly digging claws. The tail is a thick
banded cone that is much heavier than an armadillo's. The skin between the plates has a large
hexagon scale texture that looks reptilian. The body does read as heavy and low, which is
right.

**r03 -> b11-sea-turtle (confidence: high).** A domed scute-grid shell, a beaked head and four
flippers. Closest rival: b19 (a shell), dismissed because of the flippers and the beak. What is
wrong: the flippers are flat, razor-thin boards with sharp leading edges, like aircraft wings.
They are very wide (1.7 m across for a 1.4 m body) and have no taper, no cupped or paddle shape
and no visible thickness in the front view, where they appear as hairlines. The rear pair are
small and point backwards at an angle that looks stuck on. The shell is a regular grid of
rectangles, like a grenade or pineapple, not the hexagonal or irregular scutes of a turtle, and
it sits like a lid on a rounded body that sticks out behind it. The head is a heavy parrot-like
beak on a short neck with cartoon eyes. The neck is short and the head is held low and
forward. It looks fine for a swimming turtle in plan view, but the profile is too flat and long
(48 cm tall).

**r04 -> b05-cerberus (confidence: high).** Three heads fanned on necks from the chest of a
four-legged dark-furred body with paws, the heads with fangs and pointed ears. Closest rival:
b04 hydra (several heads on necks), dismissed because there are three heads, a black coat, paws
and a hound body. What is wrong: the black coat is so dark that the sheet is hard to read; form
detail is lost on every view. The heads are blunt, tapir- or bear-like, with small cone ears that
look like horns, not a hound's. The necks are short, thick and curve like a camel's, so the three
heads sit together and overlap in the side view, and the centre head is low and hidden in the
front view. The fur is a fuzzy fringe along the silhouette. The body is low and thick, with short
legs and a thick conical tail, more bear or anteater than hound. The paws are present but blobby.
Eyes are a small red dot with a pale ring. A single white fang per head is visible, which is
good.

**r05 -> b06-kraken (confidence: high).** A bulbous mantle with glowing cyan dots, two big dark
eyes with cyan slit pupils where the mantle meets the head, and eight long curling tentacles with
thin tips (four long and four shorter ones in the top view). Closest rival: b20 glow slug
(cyan glowing spots), dismissed because of the tentacles and the eyes. What is wrong: the eyes
are not "huge" next to a 4 m creature; they are sphere bumps at about a fifth of the mantle
width. The mantle is a long egg, only mildly bulbous. The tentacles are thin and spidery against
the body and have no suckers visible, though they are purple and mottled. The whole animal lies
flat on the ground plane (4.4 m wide, but the front view shows a flat sheet of splayed
tentacles), so it does not look like it swims or floats. The tentacles also do not meet the body
in an obvious ring around a beak or mouth, and no mouth is visible at all.

**r06 -> b07-reef-shark (confidence: high).** A grey-over-white body with a countershaded belly,
a tall swept-back dorsal fin, two pectoral fins, a tall tail fin and a row of small teeth in a
wide mouth. Closest rival: b06 kraken or b11 sea turtle (water creatures), dismissed because of
the fins and teeth. What is wrong: the body is a big round head with an almost eel-thin tail
stalk that tapers to a pencil, which does not look like a shark; the thickest part is just
behind the head and there is no muscular mid-body. The tail fin is a single tall triangle with no
real lower lobe, and it is thin like paper. The dorsal fin has a mesh pattern and is set far
forward, leaning back like a sail. The pectoral fins are very thin horizontal blades and look
tiny in the side view. Only one small pelvic or pectoral fin is visible under the belly. The
head has a cartoon white eye, a blunt rounded snout and no gill slits. The teeth are small
and cream-coloured, in one row on top and bottom, and read as "rows of teeth" only barely.

**r07 -> b02-wyvern (confidence: high).** Two legs, a long neck and tail, folded wings carried
where the arms would be (the wings are the arms), swept-back horns, and a small curved red
stinger on the tail tip. Closest rival: b01 dragon (same family, but it has four legs) and b03
bat (two legs and wing arms, but furred). What is wrong: the wings are barely readable. Folded,
they look like thin sticks with a comb of rays dangling beside the body, and they carry almost no
membrane, so they do not look like arms that are also wings. The legs are short and thin for a
4.5 m, 1.5 m tall body, and only one is visible in the side view. The torso is slender and
the neck is thick and long like a sauropod's, held up in front, with the tail stretching out
straight behind like a pole. The stinger is a small dark-red hook that is easy to miss. The skin
is green with dark banding and looks scaly, but there is no belly or scale detail on the wings.

**r08 -> b17-two-tailed-fox (confidence: high).** Orange fur with a white bib, a pointed head
with upright ears, and two bushy tails that fork from the base (clear in the top and front
views). Closest rival: b05 cerberus (a four-legged canine, but black with three heads),
dismissed immediately. What is wrong: the legs are bare, dark-brown tubes without fur, ending
in no distinct paws; the prompt asks for soft fur and paws. The fur is a stippled noise fringe
around the silhouette that reads as sparkle rather than soft fur. The snout is long, narrow and
lizard-like, with bared fangs sticking out of the lip, rather than a fox's gentle muzzle. The two
tails both start at the same point and spread in a V like two leaves or wings; they look flat
and wide, with no taper or white tip, and are held up and back. The body is a long tube with a
thin neck. The ears are small cones.

**r09 -> b18-griffin (confidence: high).** An eagle head with a big hooked yellow beak on a white
feathered head, feathered wings folded on the back, yellow taloned feet at the front and a tan
lion-like body and tuft-tipped tail. It is the only winged quadruped with a bird's head.
Closest rival: b01 dragon or b02 wyvern (winged), dismissed by the head and the feathers. What
is wrong: the beak is oversized and bulbous, so the head looks more like a toucan, parrot or dodo
than an eagle. The "lion's body" is thin and low, with bird-like hind legs ending in talons
and no lion haunches or paws; only the tail tuft says lion. The wings are short stubs folded
flat on the back, with rows of blocky overlapping feathers that could not lift the body. The
neck is a thick, short pillar. The fur on the body is fuzzy and the whole animal reads as a big
bird on four legs, not a hybrid.

**r10 -> b08-cave-spider (confidence: high).** Eight legs, a small head with two bright
lime-green fangs and a big smooth abdomen with a mottled brown camouflage pattern. Closest rival:
b09 scorpion king (eight limbs), dismissed because it has fangs and no pincers or sting. What is
wrong: the abdomen is glossy and egg-like, with large mirror highlights, so it looks like a
plastic ball. The fangs are a neon green that does not fit a cave spider and are large flat
blades. The front part is a long narrow head and thorax, ant-like, with a small smile and only a
few tiny black eyes, rather than a compact cephalothorax. The legs are thin and have three plain
segments with no hair or joint knees, all splayed evenly with no pedipalps. Colours are
pale-cave-brown, which fits.

**r11 -> b14-stegosaur (confidence: high).** A row of tall leaf-shaped plates down the back
(orange-red gradient, alternating left and right in the top view), a green body with a small
head and a tail with pale spikes in pairs. Closest rival: b15 sail-back (a back crest), but the
plates are separate and the tail has spikes. What is wrong: the plates are very thin, like paper,
and in the side view they overlap into a fan and are all about the same size, with no clear
large plates in the middle and small ones at the ends. The tail spikes are small and sit at the
very end, as three pairs of thorns on a thin stem pointing sideways, not large thagomizer
spikes. The head is tiny and blunt with a plain mouth line and no beak. The hind legs are
long and the shoulders low, which is right, but the body is thin and the neck long, and it
looks like a sauropod-stegosaur mix. The front view shows the plates as a narrow V.

**r12 -> b01-dragon (confidence: high).** A red-scaled body on four legs, swept-back black
horns, a ridge of pale spikes down the tail and folded ribbed wings on the back. Closest rival:
b02 wyvern (similar vocabulary, but two legs and green). What is wrong: the wings sit folded and
look like spiked ribs along the back, so "bat-like" shows only when seen from the front, and
even then the span (2.2 m) is small for a 4.9 m dragon. The fingers are straight rays and
the membrane is thin, not a bat's curved fingers. The neck is snake-thin and long, the body
slim, the legs short and the tail a very long thin whip, so it reads as a lizard with wings
rather than a heavy dragon. The scales are good on the head and neck. The tail spikes are
cream and run from the hips to the tip; they are the best-executed prompt item. The head is
small with fangs and a smiling mouth.

**r13 -> b15-sail-back (confidence: high).** A tall fan-shaped sail of red skin stretched over
pale spines on the back of a banded, sprawling reptile (it looks like a Dimetrodon). Closest
rival: b14 stegosaur or b13 frilled lizard, dismissed because of the sail and the sprawl. What is
wrong: the sail is correct but its base is short, and it sits on the shoulders only; it stands
upright with a very regular outline like a hand fan. The front view shows it as a single thin
spike, which is fine. The head is a blunt snout with two huge, outward-pointing fangs and a front
paw tucked under the jaw in the head view, which looks like a bug. The legs sprawl widely with
elbows out, and the belly is low. The tail is long, tapering and banded. The skin pattern of
stripes looks good.

**r14 -> b12-quillback-boar (confidence: high).** A heavy dark brown beast with long cream
quills down the back, two upturned tusks from the lower jaw and dark hooves. Closest rival: none;
the quills and tusks are unique. What is wrong: the build is not boar-like. The legs are long
and thin like a deer's, the body slim and flat-sided, and the head is a flat tapir or rhino shape
with no pig snout disc. The quills radiate evenly in all directions over the whole back like a
porcupine's, with the same length everywhere, not a mane of quills along the spine. The "old
scars" are pale, leaf-shaped decals on the flanks and rump that read as a pattern, not as
healed scars. The hooves are black blocks that look more like clawed toes than cloven hooves
in the front view. The tail is a tiny stub with a few quills.

**r15 -> b13-frilled-lizard (confidence: medium).** A low, banded tan lizard with a long tail and
a rust-coloured fan of membrane and spines at the neck. Closest rival: b15 sail-back (a reptile
with a membrane), dismissed because the membrane is at the neck and the animal is small and low
(19 cm tall). What is wrong: the frill is shown closed and is small, a narrow fan hanging under
the throat like a dewlap in the front view, so "wide frill around its neck" is not shown,
and it does not wrap around the neck on both sides. The animal sprawls flat, with the front legs
out like a frog's in a push-up, and the belly is almost on the ground, so it does not stand up
like a real frilled lizard. The head is a smooth blunt snout with a wide grin of tiny teeth. The
banded pattern and the long thin tail are good. Only the frill separates it from a generic
lizard.

**r16 -> b03-giant-bat (confidence: high).** Dark brown fur, very big pointed ears and folded
wings of dark membrane along the sides. Closest rival: b02 wyvern (two legs and wing arms),
dismissed because of the fur and the ears. What is wrong: the wings are barely leathery. They
are folded and trail behind as thin ribbons and rays with little membrane, and the top view
shows them as narrow strips (64 cm across), so it does not look "giant" or like a bat wing with
fingers. The body stands on two thin, short legs like a rodent, held nearly horizontal. The head
is a round fur ball with a small dot nose, tiny eyes and two small fangs, and has no bat face or
muzzle. The fur is a noisy fringe. The ears are the best part, large and pointed and set wide.
The wing arms are short sticks at the shoulder.

**r17 -> b20-glow-slug (confidence: high).** A glossy, slimy green body with cyan glowing spots
and two eye stalks with large cream eyeballs and yellow irises. Closest rival: b06 kraken (glowing
cyan dots), dismissed because of the eye stalks and the slug body. What is wrong: the body is a
smooth spindle that tapers at both ends, with a circular cross-section (the front view is a
disc with concentric rings), so there is no flat foot, no mantle, no head or neck, and no
tentacles on the head. There are only two stalks, which are thin and short compared with the
body, and the eyes are cartoon-sized. The glow spots are evenly sprinkled on the body and
there is no mucus trail or wet sheen beyond the gloss. The 2.3 m body has no segmentation or
wrinkles, so it reads as a green bean or a leech.

**r18 -> b04-hydra (confidence: high).** A four-legged body with five long necks, each ending in
a toothed head. Closest rival: b05 cerberus (several heads), dismissed because there are five
heads on long necks. What is wrong: the necks are five identical, stiff columns of the same
thickness fanned from one point at the shoulders, rising straight up like organ pipes, not
sinuous or snaking. In the side view they stack into one line, and the heads all face the same
direction with the same pose. The body is a crocodile or lizard shape with short, stubby legs and
a thick conical tail, so it reads as long and low and not "heavy". The heads are small
with a snout and a row of teeth and no variation between them. The pale underside scales on the
necks are nice. The necks come out of the back of the body above the shoulders rather than from
the chest and neck base.

**r19 -> b09-scorpion-king (confidence: high).** A dark segmented body with gold bands, six legs
(three pairs in the top view), two arms with pincers held forward, and a stinger tail with a
golden barb curled up and over the back. A gold crest of spikes sits on the head like a crown.
Closest rival: b08 cave spider (eight limbs), dismissed because of the pincers and the tail. What
is wrong: the armour is only on the abdomen as segment plates and the head carapace is a smooth
dome with four small black eyes, so the "armoured back" is only half done. The pincers are
thick, blunt tongs held low and straight out like crab claws, and they sit on thick
upper arms. The tail is a single thin tube with little segmentation and the stinger is a small
gold hook. The legs are thin and spidery. The golden crest of spikes is the only "king" cue and
looks like a mohawk.

**r20 -> b16-giant-moth (confidence: high).** A furry, segmented body, thin antennae with side
strands, big wings with a brown and cream pattern with dark eyespots, six legs. Closest rival:
b03 giant bat (wings, fur), dismissed because of the six legs, antennae and insect pattern.
What is wrong: the wings read as one flat triangular sheet with a seam down the middle, not
"four broad wings". The fore and hind wings are not separated, and from the side they are
paper-thin planes sticking out behind the body, held horizontally. One wing panel has a blown-out
white specular glare in the 3/4 view. The antennae are thin curved whips with only a few
comb strands, not the broad feathery plumes of a moth. The legs are long, thick and spidery,
like a grasshopper's, and stand wide. The fur is rendered as dotted noise, and the head is a
hairy ball with big yellow eyes and no proboscis. The wing pattern (brown with cream blotches
and black eyespots) is the strongest part.
