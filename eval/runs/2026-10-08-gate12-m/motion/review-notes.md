# Motion review notes

Blind review of m01 to m10. Matches first, then a paragraph per filmstrip. Comments on the
filmstrip tool itself are at the end.

**m01 -> m09-bear-hit-die (confidence: high).** A heavy, furry quadruped standing, with two events stamped at 1.02 s and one at 4.02 s. In frame 4 (1.31 s) the body dips and the head pushes forward and down, a flinch or stagger. By frame 5 it is upright again. From frame 11 (4.34 s) the body sinks, and by frame 12 (4.77 s) it is down. Frames 13 to 16 are a static corpse. Only the hit/die pair fits that shape, and nothing else in the set has a stagger-then-collapse timeline. Closest rival: none really (the cat and hydra also have target events, but neither falls over). Problems: the stagger shows in a single sampled frame, so the hit reads as a small dip (about 60 cm of travel) and not a stagger. From a side view I cannot see any push away from the hit side. The collapse is quick, about 0.75 s from upright to flat, and the creature ends tucked on its belly with the legs folded under it and the head sunk into the fur mass. It reads as lying down to rest rather than as a death, and the head is hard to find in frames 13 to 16. The silhouette (stubby, plump, small head) is not obviously a bear, but that is the model, not the motion. The two events at 1.02 s are printed on top of each other.

**m02 -> m01-horse-gallop (confidence: high).** A long-necked, long-legged quadruped with a mane, 88 m covered in 8 s (about 11 m/s, close to the 12 m/s task). The legs alternate between a gathered pose with all four feet bunched under the belly (frames 3 and 6), a reaching pose with the fore legs stretched forward and the hind legs trailing (frames 2, 5 and 8), and a mixed pose (4 and 7). That is a suspended gallop. Closest rival: the raptor-run (also a fast run), but that one is two-legged. Problems: the frame interval of 1.14 s aliases the stride, since frames 3 and 6 are the same pose, so the strip cannot show the footfall order or the cycle length. There is no footfall diagram under it, only an event timeline, so the three-beat order cannot be checked. The spine looks rigid. The torso stays a level sausage in the gathered and the extended pose, with no arching, no bounce and a constant head height. The tail is a stiff cone pointing down at about 45 degrees in every frame instead of streaming behind, and the mane does not move. The two events at 0.26 and 0.57 s, and frame 1 already showing a mid-stride pose, suggest the animal goes from rest to full gallop in about half a second, which is far too abrupt. It is about 8 percent slower than the task speed.

**m03 -> m02-raptor-run (confidence: high).** The only biped: a long horizontal tail, small forearms held forward, a striped body and two legs. It covers 76 m in 10 s (7.6 m/s, task 8). Frames show a long, deeply bent-knee stride with the body low, and a bunched-legs pose in frame 10. Closest rival: horse-gallop, but the raptor has two legs and a tail counterweight. Problems: frame 1 (t = 0) is an upright, straight-legged stand, then the creature drops into a deep crouch for the rest of the run (the single event is at 0.28 s), so the stand-to-run change is nearly instant. The hips sit very low and the knees are bent a lot, which looks like a sitting run, not a springy one. The forearms hold one fixed pose in every frame and do not move at all. The tail is a straight rigid rod at the same angle in every frame. The side view cannot show lateral sway, but there is no vertical bob either. The 1.1 s frame interval aliases the stride and there is no footfall diagram, so I cannot check for a flight phase or even alternation. The far leg is drawn in a pale colour that looks like a lighting or shading mismatch.

**m04 -> m03-cat-pounce (confidence: high).** A spotted tawny big cat, walking forward (frames 1 to 3), dropping into a crouch (frame 4, 0.68 s), then leaving the ground (frames 5 and 6, the shadow detached), landing (frame 7) and standing over a small orange target on the ground. Events at 0.86 s (the leap) and 1.38 s (the landing) bracket the airborne phase. Closest rival: hydra-bite-left, which also has an orange target ball, but that one is stationary, has no flight and has heads. Problems: the airborne phase lasts only 0.5 s. The legs stay tucked under the body in the air instead of reaching out (no stretched-out launch and no forward-reaching landing), and the body just tilts nose-down. After landing (frame 8 on), frames 9 to 16 (2 s) are a frozen pose, the head lowered over the ball, with no swipe, bite or pin. The cat does not settle or shift weight. "Walked 2.92 m" against a target 4 m ahead means it ended about 1 m short, or the target is closer than the task says. The crouch before the leap is one frame (0.68 s) and there is no visible wind-up. The target floats as a ball at nose level when the cat lands, with no contact or reaction from it.

**m05 -> m07-moth-hover (confidence: high).** An insect with antennae, striped abdomen and big wings, starting on the ground (frame 1, wings flat over the back, six legs down) and rising by frame 2 (0.73 s, the shadow detached). It then holds a tilted hover with the wings in different stroke positions for the rest of the 8 s while "walked 0.00 m". Three events are stamped at 0.65 s (takeoff and the start of hover). Closest rival: dragon-flight (it also takes off), but the dragon is a vertebrate with long wings, travels 682 m, and ends by landing. Problems: the body pitch keeps changing. It is almost vertical (about 75 degrees, abdomen hanging) in frames 3 to 5, around 30 to 40 degrees from frame 6, and still not steady at 8 s, so the hover never looks settled and the first seconds look like a tail-sit. The wings are enormous relative to the body and are seen edge-on or as a single flat sheet in many frames. Frame 5 shows one wing as a bright pale-lavender sheet that looks like a specular or shading glitch. The wing membrane shows a visible hatch pattern. The legs dangle loose and nothing tucks them away. The filmstrip camera follows the moth, so the ground is out of view from frame 4 on and neither the hover height nor the drift can be read. The 0.73 s sample interval aliases the wingbeat. The three 0.65 s labels overprint into an unreadable smear.

**m06 -> m08-hydra-bite-left (confidence: high).** Green, spined quadruped with several heads on a thick, bundled neck, standing still (walked 0 cm). An orange target ball hangs at chest height on the camera side, which is the creature's left, since it faces image-left. One event at 0.78 s. In frames 4 and 5 (0.66 and 0.87 s) the head cluster dips forward and down with the mouths open (red inside), then it returns to the raised pose by frame 6 (1.09 s) and holds until the end. Closest rival: cat-pounce (a target ball too), but the cat leaps and has one head. Problems: the heads move as one bundle. The neck and heads are so tightly stacked that they read as a single thick neck with a cluster at the top, not as several necks fanned out. The strike does not show one nearest head biting while the others stay raised. In frame 5 the heads end below and in front of the ball, around knee height and about 0.4 m ahead of it, so in this view the bite looks like it misses or goes at the ground in front of the target. The ball floats at about chest height with no support and does not react. The body does not turn or step toward the target, and the legs stay frozen in all 12 frames. The whole bite lasts about 0.5 s, so it is a snap with no wind-up. The side view cannot confirm the head reaches left, only that the ball is on that side.

**m07 -> m04-shark-turn (confidence: high).** A top-down view in plain blue, with a streamlined fish-shaped body, two swept pectoral fins and a tapering tail. Frames 1 to 7 (0 to 8.8 s) are a steady straight cruise with the tail tip swinging a little side to side. Frame 8 (10.27 s) is mid-turn with the body rotated about 20 degrees. Frames 9 to 16 show it turned 90 degrees, heading off to one side, with the tail still swinging. There is no event timeline. Closest rival: turtle-dive, also seen from above in water, but that one has a shell, large flippers, and a vertical-depth task with events. Problems: the body stays a straight rigid rod through the turn. Only the tail bends, with no body curve into the turn, no banking that I can see, and the turn is a 90 degree swing that appears between frames 8 and 9 (1.5 s apart), so it may be faster than a 3 m shark would manage. The tail swing is small and the tail has no visible caudal fin from above, ending in a bare point (frame 8 shows a faint trailing line at the tip). A hard-edged dark rhomboid patch on the back reads as a rendering artifact, not a gill or a pattern. The pectoral fins are fixed at one angle in every frame. The camera follows the shark, so the 32 m of travel (about 1.5 m/s, slow for a cruising shark) cannot be seen.

**m08 -> m10-turtle-dive (confidence: high).** A top-down view of a shell with a brick-like scute lattice and two long, swept flippers plus tiny rear flippers. Frame 1 is bright and golden, frames 2 to 7 are grey-blue (tinted by the water) and then frames 8 to 12 show only the central crown in gold. Two events, at 14.34 and 29.41 s, split the 32 s run into a dive and a return. In frame 2 (2.9 s) the shell is short and foreshortened, so it is pitched steeply nose-down. It is level and steady through frames 3 to 6, then foreshortened again in frame 7 (17.5 s) as it climbs. Closest rival: shark-turn (also top-down in open water), but that has no shell, no tint change and no events. Problems: the return does not look complete. At the end (frames 9 to 12) only the crown of the shell is bright, and the rim and flippers stay tinted, while in frame 1 the whole shell is bright. If bright means above the water, the start pose floats clearly higher than the end pose, so "back up to the surface" ends with the shell mostly awash, or the turtle is still a little below the surface. Also frames 9 to 12 do not change at all across 9 s, so the last third of the run is idle. The long flippers are straight, stiff blades held at nearly the same angle in frames 3 to 6 and again in 8 to 12, so no flapping or rowing stroke is visible (aliasing could explain this, but nothing in the strip shows a stroke). The scutes are a rectangular brick grid, not turtle-shaped plates. The vertical motion is hard to judge from above: the camera follows the animal, so the depth of 3 m cannot be read from the frames, only guessed from the tint.

**m09 -> m06-dragon-flight (confidence: high).** A red dragon with huge membrane wings. Frame 1 stands on flat ground (tiny in the frame). Frame 2 (3.1 s) climbs steeply with the wings mid-downstroke. Frames 3 to 13 are banked circling flight with the wings up, spread or in a V. Frame 14 (39.9 s) flares with the wings wide, and frames 15 and 16 stand on a tilted ground plane with the wings folded along the back, which is the slope landing. Events at 0.36 s (three at once), 14.02, 26.02 and 42.35 s (three at once) fit takeoff, circling, glide and landing. 682 m in 46 s is about 15 m/s. Closest rival: moth-hover (a takeoff), but only the dragon travels, glides and lands on a slope. Problems: the dragon is very small in frames 1, 8, 12 and 13, so the wing stroke and glide pose are hard to judge. In frame 7 (18.4 s) the grey ground band sits above the dragon, which means the camera is rolled or the dragon is below the ground plane, and I cannot tell which. Frames 3 to 5 and 10 to 12 show the wing planes edge-on or at near-vertical angles that look like a bank of 60 degrees or more with no change in the body roll. The wing membrane shows a visible grid pattern. At landing, frame 14 is still flying and frame 15 (3 s later) is already standing and settled, so the touchdown is missing from the strip. On the slope the body seems to stand parallel to the ground with the legs splayed on all four feet and the neck up, which is plausible, but the front feet look higher than the rear and I could not see any weight shift. The tail drags in a straight rigid line in the flight frames.

**m10 -> m05-crocodile-lake (confidence: high).** A long, low reptile with striped tail walking in (frame 1, then frame 2 at the water's edge), going nose-first into a blue lake (frame 3, tilted, tail up), swimming with the body mostly submerged (frames 4 to 7), climbing out on the far side (frame 8, front legs on land, tail in water), and then walking on dry ground (frames 9 to 12). Events at 3.09 s (two), 11.14 s (two) and 17.81 s fit entering, leaving and stopping. Closest rival: turtle-dive (water), but that is a shell seen from above and this is a side view with a bank and a lake edge. Problems: the entry at frame 3 (3.5 s) is a steep nose-down tilt with the tail raised, as if falling into a pit, instead of wading in gradually. In the swim (frames 4 to 7) the body is a straight, stiff plank with legs dangling or trailing, and no lateral undulation can be seen from the side. There is no visible bob or wake, and the legs do not paddle. The water is a flat translucent ellipse that the body shows through, so I cannot tell whether the body sits at the right depth. At the exit (frame 8) the head and front legs are on land while the tail is still deep in the water, then the creature is fully out by frame 9 with no pause. Frames 10 to 12 (15.5 to 19 s) show an identical pose, so it is standing still or the sampling aliased the walk, with the final event at 17.81 s unclear.

## Notes on the tool

- The footfall diagram is missing under m02 and m03 (only the event timeline is shown). For gait tasks (gallop, run) it is the one thing that would let a reviewer judge the footfall order and any flight phase.
- The frame interval for the gait strips (1.1 s) is far longer than a stride, so the poses alias and look like a slow cycle. A strip sampled within one stride, or a second strip at a short interval, would make the gait readable.
- Events stamped at the same time (m01, m05, m09, m10) print on top of each other. Stacking or merging them into one label would fix it.
- The chase camera hides speed, height and depth (shark, moth, turtle, dragon). A ground or depth reference (a grid, a ruler or a height label) would let a reviewer judge hover height, dive depth and glide altitude.
- Several strips show a final long run of identical frames (m01 frames 13 to 16, m04 frames 9 to 16, m08 frames 9 to 12, m10 frames 10 to 12). Cutting the idle tail, or sampling more densely around the events, would show more of the actual motion.
