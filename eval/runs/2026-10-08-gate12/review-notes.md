# Blind review notes, gate 12

All twenty renders were matched one-to-one from the six-view sheets and `prompts.md` only. No render was ambiguous enough to need guessing. Where a rival is named, it is the second-best prompt for that render. Notes below are about what the tool should fix, so they are critical on purpose.

**r01 -> p08-boar (confidence: high).** Heavy brown quadruped, 1.6 m long and 72 cm tall, with a very big head fused to the body, short legs, two tusks and a tiny tail stub at the rear (visible as a small tick in the side view). Closest rival: p19-rhino (horn-like tusks, stocky body) and p13-cave-bear (brown and bulky), but there is no nose horn and no claws. Wrong or missing: the tusks leave the snout nearly horizontally (front and top views show them sticking out sideways like handlebars) instead of curving up from the lower jaw, and one reads as a long straight spike in the side and 3/4 views. The snout is a blunt wedge with no pig disc or nostrils. The ears are sharp cones that read as small horns. A row of black spines runs down the back, which the prompt did not ask for (it makes the creature read as nightmare hound or spiked lizard). The legs are thin tubes under a barrel body and the front legs are visibly bent, so it looks like it is about to collapse. The hooves are tiny dark nubs.

**r02 -> p01-wolf (confidence: high).** Grey fur, long tapering snout, pointed ears, a visible fang, paler legs and underside, long tail. Closest rival: p14-leopard-stalker (fur and long body), but no spots or slit pupils. Wrong or missing: the proportions are not wolf. The body is long and narrow (36 cm wide, 1.9 m long) with long spindly digitigrade legs that kink backwards like a theropod or kangaroo, and the head hangs low on a thin neck. The tail is not bushy: it is a long, smooth continuation of the torso with no visible base, so body plus tail reads as one sausage. The shell fur renders as sparse, spiky, sparkly fuzz that looks like frost rather than dense fur. The paler belly is only clear on the lower legs ("socks"), not along the belly in the side view. The muzzle is very flat and long, more like a crocodile or fox than a wolf, and the chest is shallow (no deep ribcage).

**r03 -> p20-nightmare-hound (confidence: high).** Black skin, small glowing red eyes, a row of tall jagged pale spines from the neck down the back and along the tail, a cluster of big fangs, brownish grime on the lower legs and belly. Closest rival: p02-spiked-lizard (row of back spikes), but this has a whip tail, not a club, and has a canine body. Wrong or missing: the spines are uniform, evenly spaced cones, so they look like a stegosaur or a mohawk (the front view shows one huge central spike) and not "jagged". The grime is a soft brown gradient up the legs, not dirt, and the faint purple scratch lines on the flank are barely readable. The fangs are a messy clump of white shards at the snout tip. The head is rat-like, with a thin neck and long thin legs that make it look small and weak for a "nightmare hound". The tail is a thin spiked whip. Red eyes are small, so the glow does not read from far away.

In the head view only one is clearly visible and in the top view they look like antennae, so "two mandible-like horns" does not read at a glance. The long whip antennae sweep back from the top of the head and look wrong. The eyes are cartoon white balls with black pupils stuck on the front sides of the head, which is out of style for an ant. ## Cross-cutting observations

- Heads and mouths: many creatures share the same wide, grinning, frog-like mouth line and large side-mounted eyes (r05, r09, r14, r18, r19). Faces differ little between species.
- Torsos: bipeds (r08, r11, r14) are tubes with no neck, shoulders, waist or hips, and hands and feet are tiny. This is the weakest anatomy in the set.
- Fangs and tusks are often clusters of white shards (r03, r11, r14) or thick spikes pointing the wrong way (r01, r13).
- Serpents (r06, r12, r13) lie straight along the ground with no S-curve, and the cobra rear is an L-kink with flat cardboard hood flaps.
- Shell fur (r02, r09) adds sparkle noise that hides both the spots and the fur direction, and reads thin from a distance.
- Lengths and thicknesses: long things are often a thin uniform tube (r05 tail, r12 and r13 bodies, r02 tail), so "giant" and "bushy" do not show as girth.
