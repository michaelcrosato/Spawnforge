# Design: several heads and split tails (milestone 9.1)

7.3 put `body.neck.count`, `spread`, `body.tail.count`, `spread` and `forkAt` in the format, and
7.4 turned the rig's heads and tails into lists, each holding one entry. 9.1 builds the rest.
Several heads are meshed, cut for their mouths, and given their own eyes, teeth and parts; tails
split from the torso or fork partway. Motion drives each head and tail. Rule one: at count 1
every creature compiles to exactly today's mesh, so the goldens do not move.

## Today

- `buildSkeleton` builds one neck, head and jaw (in the creature's YZ plane), then one tail.
  The rig holds `heads: [{ id: 'head' }]` and `tails: [{ id: 'tail' }]`, plus one tail spring
  chain. It also returns one mouth.
- `notBuilt` reports `count > 1` and parts on `head.L1` and the like, and `buildable` drops them.
  Validation already names instances and warns `heads_overlap`.
- Downstream code already loops over `rig.heads`:
  - `refineHeads`, `cutMouth`, `mouthInside`, `lipLine` and the sockets (`head.L1`, `mouth.L1`);
  - eyes find their head through bone parents;
  - mouth-slot parts find their head's mouth (`headOf`);
  - actions use the head nearest their target, which lunges while every head looks.

## Heads

- **One builder, run per instance.** The neck, head, jaw, head details and mouth shape move
  into one function. It builds an instance in today's frame, rooted at the torso's front, then
  turns everything it made (bone ends, up vectors, mass points) about the vertical axis through
  its root and shifts it sideways. The main head has no turn and no shift at count 1, so its
  bones are today's to the bit.
- **The fan.** Instance `i` of `n` sits at `k = i − (n − 1)/2`:
  - its root shifts `k × 1.2 × chest radius / (n − 1)` along X, across the chest's front (the
    layout `headSpacing` already assumes for `heads_overlap`);
  - its neck turns `k × spread / (n − 1)` degrees about the vertical;
  - pitch, curve, length and the head are shared.
  - With an even count, the main head (just left of the middle) is turned too.
  - `dorsalUp` is only used before the turn, so it never sees a sideways forward.
- **Names.**
  - The main instance keeps `neck.0…`, `head`, `jaw`, `jaw.helper`.
  - The others put the instance suffix after the section: `neck.L1.0…`, `head.L1`, `jaw.L1`,
    `jaw.L1.helper`.
  - Chains and section paths are `neck.L1`, `head.L1` and `jaw.L1`, so parts and limbs address
    them as the format says.
- **Mouths and refinement.**
  - Each head gets its own `mouthShape` and head details. Brows sit over that head's eyes, which
    come from the parts copied onto it.
  - `refineHeads` splits its allowance by subtracting what earlier heads used, and counts the
    mouth insides of the heads still to come (today's share never does). So five heads stay
    within the skin limit, each a little coarser than a lone head.
- **Body coordinates.** Extra necks, heads and jaws take the axis values of their counterpart on
  the main instance, bone for bone. They are not on the spine path, which would otherwise give
  each one a single constant. So patterns lie the same way on every head.
- **The spine path** (snout to tail for patterns and `bodyCoordinates`) uses the main head, as
  it does today.

## Parts on several heads

- **Copies.** `expandCreature` copies every part whose `on` is `head`, `jaw` or `neck`, or a part
  on such a part at any depth, once per extra instance.
  - A copy's id is the part's id plus the instance suffix, then the side: `horns.L1.L`. Its `on`
    names the instance (`head.L1`) or the copied parent (`horns.L1.L`).
  - Copies keep the part's `baseId`, so they draw from the same random stream and every head
    is identical, as the format promises.
- **One head only.** A part written `on: "head.R1"` stays on that head.
- **Limbs** on the neck stay on the main neck.
- **Errors.** `notBuilt` loses its count and instance rows; `buildable` no longer drops extra
  instances.

## Tails

- **Separate tails** (`forkAt` 0):
  - Each tail is the tail builder run per instance, rooted at the hips' rear.
  - It shifts `k × 0.8 × rear radius / (n − 1)` and turns `k × spread / (n − 1)` about the
    vertical. Tails point backward, so the turn's sign is the opposite of a head's.
  - Bone names `tail.0…` for the main tail, `tail.L1.0…` for the others; chains and paths
    `tail.L1`.
- **A forked tail** (`forkAt` f > 0):
  - The trunk is the tail's first `round(f × segments)` segments, built once in today's plane.
  - From the fork, each branch continues the radius profile and curl. Each is built in today's
    plane from the fork's frame, then turned about the vertical axis through the fork.
  - The main branch is part of the main tail's chain (trunk then branch), so a count-1 tail
    keeps its one chain.
  - The other branches are chains parented to the last trunk bone, named `tail.L1.k` (k
    continuing from the fork).
  - `TailRig.bones` lists trunk then branch, with `branch` set to the first branch bone.
- **Parts on tails** copy per tail like parts on heads. On a forked tail, a part that sits wholly
  on the trunk (`to` ≤ `forkAt`) is placed once.
- **Springs.**
  - Each tail is a spring chain: the main tail's chain covers trunk and main branch, and each
    other branch's chain starts at the fork.
  - Springs run in rig order, trunk first, so a branch hangs from where the trunk swung to.
  - Legless bodies, whose main tail slithers, now spring their extra tails too.

## Motion

- **Glances.** The main head keeps today's glances. Each other head replays them late, by a
  delay of 0.4 to 1.6 s drawn from `glance:<head id>`, so the heads look about at different
  moments while a `look` or action target turns them all at once.
- **Turning.** Every neck bends into a turn, bone for bone with the main neck, so the outer heads
  do not swing into the middle one.
- **Bites** already use the nearest head (7.4); the event's `position` is that head's mouth, and
  the event gains `head` (the head's id).
- **Rearing and slither** stay on the main neck and tail. A legless body springs its extra tails
  after the slither has posed the main one.

## Analysis

- **`head_intersection`** comes from `analyze`'s motion run.
  - Capsules of one head's neck, head and jaw against another head's. A neck's first quarter is
    left out, where the necks meet the chest.
  - The overlap test is the same one `limb_intersection` uses.
  - The fix names `spread` or the neck `length` that clears it.
- **Reach.** `reach.heads` already lists every head; stats get `heads` from the count.

## Runtime and export

- Sockets `head.L1`, `mouth.L1` and so on already come per head and become nodes in the runtime
  and in `.glb` files (`socket_head_L1`).
- Hit capsules cover every body bone.

## Examples and checks

- **New examples:**
  - `hydra`: a heavy quadruped with five heads on long necks;
  - `cerberus`: three heads, paws;
  - `two-tailed-fox`: fur, paws, two tails.
- **Done when** each compiles, renders, walks the course and bites cleanly, passes `analyze`
  without `head_intersection`, and exposes per-head sockets in the runtime.
- **Tests:**
  - count 1 gives today's fingerprints;
  - names, chains and paths per instance;
  - copies of parts per head (and once on a trunk);
  - the fan's layout against `headSpacing`;
  - a bite from the nearest head;
  - every tail springs;
  - `head_intersection` fires on a crowded fan;
  - the runtime exposes `head.L1`.
- **Goldens.** Unchanged (count 1 everywhere in the examples); the new examples add their own.

## Changes while building

- **Names.** The draft prefixed instance bones (`head.L1/neck.0`). Putting the suffix after the
  section (`neck.L1.0`) keeps one rule for bones, chains and part ids, and lets code find a
  bone's main counterpart by dropping the suffix.
- **Glances.** The draft gave each head glances of its own. Heads that looked different ways at
  once crossed necks on a cerberus, so each head now follows the main head's glances late, and
  `analyze` checks the result.
- **Budget.** Splitting the allowance alone left five heads at 30.3k skin triangles; counting
  the mouth insides still to come brought them under 30k.
- **Spacing.** A cerberus with wolf-sized heads needs about `spread` 90 and necks of 0.45 to
  clear `head_intersection`; the blueprint guide's recipe says so.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| Build each instance directly in its turned frame | `dorsalUp` and the S-curve assume a forward in the YZ plane; turning after building reuses them unchanged and keeps the main head bit-identical |
| Necks from one point on the chest | 7.3 rejected it: heads crowd and the roots pinch; spreading the roots matches `headSpacing` |
| One random stream per part copy | Copies would differ; the format says the heads are identical |
| A trunk chain plus one chain per branch, including the main branch | A count-1 tail would split into two chains and move the goldens |
