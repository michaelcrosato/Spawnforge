# Design: eight legs and centaurs (milestone 9.2)

7.3 put the `octopod` and `centaur` body plans in the format, and both compile today. But
neither reads as what it is. The octopod's legs run straight out and down, and its first two
pairs pass through each other in the stride (`limb_intersection`, 2.5 cm). The centaur's upright
front is a tube that curves forward off the torso like a swan's neck. Its bones are bunched at
the base, so the arms hang from halfway up. There are no shoulders or chest, and the arms reach
down to the ground like a third pair of legs. 9.2 makes both bodies work through the skeleton,
the gaits and the controller, not just through presets. Rule one: no example in the golden set
moves.

## Eight legs

- **`tripod` on four pairs.** `legPairs: [3, 4]`. Each leg's phase offset is
  `(pair × w + 0.5 × side) mod 1`, so `w = 0.5` on four pairs gives the alternating tetrapod
  spiders use: L1 R2 L3 R4 against R1 L2 R3 L4, with four feet planted at all times. No new gait
  module is needed. At slow speeds `walk` (w = 1/4) runs a metachronal wave, back to front, with
  each side half a cycle apart. The filmstrip footfall diagrams check both.
- **Arched legs.** A sprawled leg's rest foot is placed from its length. Its hip height is 0.45
  × the length and its reach out to the side 0.65 × the length, capped at 0.84 × the length, so
  long legs stand almost straight. That is wrong for spiders, whose legs are two or three body
  lengths long and arch their knees high over a body that hangs low. The new rule caps the
  **hip height** of a sprawled leg at what a leg of `ARCH_REACH` (0.7 torso lengths) would get.
  Its spread to the side and fore and aft still grows with its length, so any extra length goes
  into a wider stance and a knee raised above the hip by the sprawl pole (up and out).
  - It is weighted by splay: none below 35°, full from 55°. Upright legs and short sprawled legs
    (every hexapod leg is at most 0.7) keep today's rest pose to the bit. A biped's slight splay
    no longer reaches it, and the goldens do not move.
- **The octopod preset** gives leg lengths the way spiders have them: 1.1, 0.95, 0.85 and 1.0,
  front to back. Front feet fan forward and hind feet back with the length (the existing
  `fore`). The hips are spread a little further along the cephalothorax (`at` 0.04 to 0.31). In
  the stride the first pair now clears the second, and the preset passes `analyze` with no
  warnings.
- **Scorpions** are the same plan with `arm` limbs carrying `hand.pincer` and a curled tail. The
  pincer is 9.4's, so the scorpion example waits for it. The spider's fangs (`mandible`) wait
  for 9.4 too.

## The upright front

7.3 decided the form: arms on the neck make the neck a torso. "Upright front" means a neck that
carries `arm` limbs (`isUprightFront`). Everything below applies only to such a neck, so other
necks are unchanged.

- **Path.**
  - Necks curve out of the torso's front: a Bézier whose first control point lies 0.4 × the
    neck's length ahead along the torso. An upright front puts it at 0.08 instead, so it rises
    nearly straight up from the horse's front, as a rider sits.
  - The curve is resampled by arc length, so its bones are equal and `at` and the radius profile
    fall where they say. Today's bones on a centaur ran 0.12 to 0.36 m: the shoulders, at `at`
    0.3, came out halfway up.
  - Other necks keep the curve's own spacing (the goldens).
- **Shoulders and chest** (the chest rule 8.1 left to 9.2). Two masses on the front's chain,
  both growing with `body.muscle` and absent at 0, like 8.1's masses:
  - a bar across the shoulders, at the arms' `at`, out to where the arms join, slightly toward
    the back: (0.42 + 0.08 s) × the local radius;
  - pectorals 0.12 below it, on the front: (0.32 + 0.08 s) × the radius, 0.38 of the half-width
    to each side.
  - The neck muscle that 8.1 runs into a quadruped's shoulders is left off, because it would
    bulge the human's lower back.
  - The radius profile carries the rest: a thin neck under the head, the ribcage, the waist and
    the hips joining the horse body.
- **Arms join at the chest's edge.** Limb roots sit 0.55 of the way out from the section's axis,
  so legs grow out of the body. Arms on an upright front sit at 0.85, so they hang clear of the
  ribs below square shoulders. Arms on a torso are unchanged.
- **The centaur preset** uses all of this:
  - a 0.72-long, `wide` front with a ten-point profile (neck 0.042, shoulders 0.14, waist 0.1,
    hips 0.125), at pitch 86;
  - arms 0.62 long at `at` 0.3 with `hand.grasp`;
  - `foot.hoof` on all four legs, `body.muscle` 0.6, and a smaller round head (0.2 by 0.08)
    with lips and a brow.

## Motion

- **Posture.**
  - The controller pitches the body for slopes and for `rear`. An upright front turns back
    against 80% of that pitch at its first bone, so it stays upright the way a rider does,
    instead of tipping with the horse body.
  - Turns bend the spine and neck about each bone's dorsal axis, which on an upright bone would
    lean it sideways. The front's bones turn about their own long axis instead, which twists
    the torso into the turn.
- **Arms swing with the legs.**
  - Bipeds keep today's swing, driven by the gait phase.
  - Arms above four or more legs swing with the opposite foreleg, as a walking person's arms
    follow their legs. The swing follows the foreleg's actual foot, measured fore and aft of
    its rest spot along the heading and scaled by the half stride, so it works for every gait,
    stops when the legs stand still, and needs no phase bookkeeping. Amplitude: 0.3 radians.
- **Arms free for actions.** A new goal, `arms` (0 to 1), raises the arms forward, up to 75°,
  toward what the head looks at, and fades the swing out as it rises.
  - `bite` sets it (0.7 at the strike), so a centaur, a troll or a raptor seizes what it bites.
  - Arms are never planted and never count as legs. 9.4's `pinch` will drive them the same way.
- **Balance.**
  - `analyze` puts the centre of mass from the mesh's volume, so the front's mass already
    counts. A test pins this: the preset's centre lies ahead of the horse body's middle and
    inside the feet.
  - A front made heavy enough tips the centaur forward. The `unbalanced` warning then names the
    upright front in its fix: lean it back, slim it, or move the forelegs forward.

## Tests

- `tripod` accepts four pairs, and its offsets on an octopod make the alternating tetrapod (two
  sets of four, on alternating sides).
- Arched legs:
  - on the octopod preset, every knee rises above its hip;
  - hexapod and biped rest poses are unchanged (the goldens).
- Upright front:
  - the bones are equal in length;
  - the shoulders (the arm roots) sit where `at` says;
  - the arms hang clear of the ribs.
- Motion:
  - the front stays within a few degrees of upright on a slope where the horse body pitches;
  - the arms swing opposite to the forelegs;
  - `bite` raises them.
- Both presets and both examples walk the terrain course (a scenario on the `course` ground):
  every point reached, feet planted without sliding, `analyze` clean.
- Balance: the centaur's centre of mass is ahead of the torso's middle; a heavy front warns
  `unbalanced` with the front named in the fix.

## Examples

- `examples/tomb-spider.json`: the octopod with banded legs, eyes and side eyes.
- `examples/grove-centaur.json`: the centaur with hooves, hands, horns and pointed ears.

Both get renders, and `render:examples` re-renders the rest (nothing else moves).

## Not in 9.2

- Mandibles, pincers and the scorpion (9.4).
- A human face: heads remain the format's animal heads, with lips and brows.
- A dynamic balance check over the stride (the centre of mass over the planted feet frame by
  frame). It would flag every trot, whose support is a line, so it needs a model of dynamic
  balance first. Later column.
