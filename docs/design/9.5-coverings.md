# Design: coverings: shells, plates, quills, frills, hoods and sails (milestone 9.5)

Plan 2's 9.5 (effort xhigh): the `area` slot and `scatter` in the geometry kit; `shell`,
`plates.row`, `armor.bands`, `quills`, `frill`, `hood` and `sail`; a `display` action that opens
frills and hoods, raises quills and sails and spreads wings on 9.4's part bones. Hard parts stay
within 20k triangles at medium, in one draw call. **Done when** turtle, stegosaur, porcupine,
frilled-lizard, cobra and sail-back examples compile and render within budgets, `display` works
on them, and the harness covers the area slot.

## Today

- Format 0.2 holds the vocabulary: the seven modules and `display` validate and are skipped
  (`planned: '9.5'`). Area parts carry `attach.area` (`back`, `belly`, `sides`, `all`) with
  `from` and `to`; `validate` rejects `area` on other slots.
- `ctx.socket(at, angle)` finds the skin round a section at a point, but only on the part's own
  side: an unmirrored part (`mirror` 0) gets straight up whatever its angle, so nothing can
  cover both flanks.
- 9.4 gave part modules bones (`hooks.bones`, `ctx.chains`), driven chains (`spring`, `jaw`,
  `grip`), `ctx.solid` (hard pieces with weights per vertex) and `ctx.sheet` (thin sheets in the
  membrane mesh, double-sided and lit through). `DrivenChain` already lists a `flare` drive.

## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Covering both flanks | `ctx.surface(at, angle)`: the skin at a signed angle (negative to the right), whatever the part's side. `ctx.area` gives the part's area as an angle band and its `from`/`to`: `back` within 70° of the top, `sides` 50°–130°, `belly` beyond 110°, `all` everything. |
| 2 | `scatter` | `ctx.scatter(spacing, { count? })`: Poisson-disk points over the area by dart throwing from the part's stream, measured on the body (along the section's length, round its radius), then placed on the skin. Deterministic, at most `count` points. |
| 3 | Shell | A grid over the area offset from the skin by `thickness` and lifted by `dome` (most at the middle), with a rim that reaches `overhang` past the area's edge and turns down. Built from the skin's own surface points, so it follows any torso and bends with the spine (each vertex carries the skin's weights there). Scutes: a central row, a row each side and marginal plates round the rim, each a slightly raised cell inset from its neighbours over a base in `seamColor`. |
| 4 | Armour bands | `bands` strips across the area from `from` to `to`, each overlapping the next by `overlap`, its back edge raised so they shingle; `scales` cuts each band into plates round the body. |
| 5 | Plates | Flat outlines (`kite`, `round`, `spike`) extruded thin with the geometry kit's `plate`, standing along the row in the body's midplane, alternating a little left and right and leaning out when `alternate`; edges take `edgeColor`. |
| 6 | Quills | Scattered over the area, spaced by `density`, lying back by `lie`; colour runs to `tipColor`. Groups of quills share a bone (about 12 along by 5 round): the base of each quill stays on the skin and its tip rides its group's bone, which `display` turns upright. |
| 7 | Frills, hoods and sails | Fans of spines on bones of their own, with skin between neighbours as a sheet in the membrane mesh (lit through, weighted across from one spine's bone to the next). Each spine is built at rest (folded back by `1 − open` of a right angle; a sail's lean back 15°) and its `flare` pose turns it open about the ring's tangent. A frill rings the neck at one point; a hood is a fan of ribs down each side of the neck, longest in its middle, with two eye marks on its back; a sail stands spines along the row. |
| 8 | The flare | A drive like 9.4's: a `flare` chain turns each bone by its `full` pose times the flare (`applyFace`, so stills can show it). The controller damps a flare value toward the new `flare` goal in about 0.15 s; `render --flare` and the MCP render's `pose.flare` show it open. |
| 9 | `display` | Needs a part that provides `display` (as the format says). Looks at the target, rears a little, opens the jaw to hiss, flares fully, spreads any wings, holds and folds back; fires `display-peak` when fully open. |
| 10 | Examples | `stone-tortoise` (a turtle: domed shell, column feet), `plated-stegosaur` (alternating kite plates, tail spikes), `porcupine` (quills over the back, fur), `frilled-lizard` (a frill on a sprawling lizard), `hooded-cobra` (a rearing serpent's hood) and `sail-back` (a sprawling dimetrodon). Each lists `display` where it can. |

## Surface sampling and scatter (decisions 1 and 2)

- `ctx.surface(at, angle)` is `socketOn(on, at, angle, 1)` with the angle signed; area parts use
  it for every point, so a shell's left and right halves come from one grid.
- Scatter measures distance in body terms, so spacing holds on thin and thick sections: along is
  the section's length between the two points' `at`, round is the radius there times the angle
  between them. Candidates are uniform in `at` and angle within the area (weighted by radius so
  thick parts get their share), up to 30 tries per point; a point is kept when no kept point is
  within `spacing`. Only kept points march to the skin.

## Shell (decision 3)

- Grid: `rows` along `from`→`to` by `cols` across the area's angle band, both scaled by
  `ctx.detail`; each grid point is the skin there, pushed out along the skin normal by
  `thickness` plus `dome × 0.35 × radius × (1 − u²)(1 − v²)` (u, v from −1 to 1 across the
  shell).
- Rim: the outermost ring is carried a further `overhang × radius` outward and down, and an
  inner skirt joins it back to just above the skin, so the shell has an edge and no gap.
- Scutes: `scutes` sets the counts (about 5 vertebral, 4 costal per side, the rest marginal);
  each scute is its cell of the grid inset by a seam, lifted a further quarter of the thickness,
  with its own normals. `scutes` 0 is a smooth shell in `color`.
- Legs leave the torso below the rim (`back` stops 70° from the top).

## Display and flare (decisions 8 and 9)

- `DrivenChain.drive` gains nothing new (`flare` was reserved in 7.3); poses are `full`.
- Order per step: as 9.4, with the flare applied with the face (after the jaw and grip).
- `display` goals: `look` at the target, `flare` 1 from 15% to 80% of the action, `wings` 1,
  `rear` 0.15, `jaw` 0.3 (a hiss), `raise` a little, `crouch` before; event `display-peak` at
  35%.

## Analysis and description

- Descriptions come from the modules' `describe` hooks (as today); `display` lists among actions.
- `measurements` gain nothing; part sizes come through `measure` as for other parts.
- No new warnings: shells and bands sit on the skin by construction; spines and quills are
  checked by the existing `part_buried`.

## Tests

- **Surface and scatter:** `surface` covers both sides symmetrically; scatter is the same twice,
  keeps its spacing (no two points closer than `spacing`), stays inside the area's band and
  `from`/`to`, and respects `count`.
- **Shell and bands:** every shell vertex is outside the skin and within `thickness + dome +
  overhang` of it; the shell follows a bent spine (its vertices carry skin weights).
- **Flare:** a frill's spine tips move outward and away from the neck at flare 1; a hood's span
  widens; quill tips rise; a sail's spines stand up. `display` fires `display-peak` with the
  flare open.
- **Harness:** every built part, area parts included, compiles with its defaults and example,
  the same twice, within time and size budgets.
- **Examples:** each compiles within budgets (parts at most 20k triangles at medium), analyses
  without warnings and renders.

## Budgets

A shell is about 2k triangles plus 100 per scute; quills about 20 each, capped at 400; a frill or
sail about 30 per spine plus its sheet. Part bones: up to 60 quill groups, one per spine.

## Not in 9.5

Shells as hit volumes, a plastron (belly shell; `area: "belly"` gives one), animated scales
(pangolins rolling up), quills that fly off.

## As built

- **Surface lookups**: finding the skin costs up to 60 field evaluations, so `ctx.surface` takes
  a neighbouring socket and brackets from its depth (about a third of the work), and `skinGrid`
  (in the pack's `_cover.ts`) samples every row along the body (where muscle swells and narrows
  it) but at most 13 points round it, blending positions, normals and weights between.
- **Shell**: the dome rises with `(1 − u²)^0.7 (1 − v²)^0.7` (a carapace's steep edge), so the
  underside sinks along the skin's normal, not the top's, and the rim carries on along the skin's
  tangent past the edge (following the dome there pushed it into the flanks). The rim droops only
  over the flanks. Scutes cover the rim too: marginals down each side and across each end.
- **Quills**: a group's bone turns its quills about an axis across the body, so the groups are
  thin along it (24 by 5 round) and each quill is rigid on its group's bone; blending a quill's
  base onto the skin kinked it.
- **Hood**: the ribs leave the neck at 80° from the top, lie back along the neck at rest and
  swing forward to open in the hood's own plane (folding them toward the throat made a cup); the
  eye marks face along that plane's normal, which the turn keeps. The default `at` moved to 0.08,
  just behind the head.
- **Sail**: its spines lean back 15° at rest and stand up in `display`.
- **`from`/`to` defaults**: expansion took 0 and 1 whatever the module said; it now takes the
  module's (`plates.row` 0.2–0.8, `sail` 0.25–0.6, `quills` 0.3–0.9). No built part relied on
  it.
- **Descriptions**: modules name the part (`quills`, `a frill`); the description adds where
  (area parts on the torso or spine say `on its back`, `on its sides`, `on its belly` or `all
  over it`).
- **Not done**: a plastron of its own, scutes shaped as polygons rather than cells of the grid.
