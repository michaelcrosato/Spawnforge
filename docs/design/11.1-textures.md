# Design: texture maps from UV atlases (milestone 11.1)

Exports carry the creature in vertex colours today: albedo only, one roughness per mesh, no
relief, no glow, blurred pupils and veins. 11.1 unwraps every mesh into a UV atlas at export and
bakes the live material into texture maps, so an exported creature looks in any engine as it
does live, within what glTF can carry. The plan names one max step: deciding, per live effect
glTF cannot carry as such, what it becomes. That comes first. The first draft was reviewed
adversarially (16 findings, 2 blockers); this is the answered design, and the findings are at
the end.

Rule one: the live creature does not change. The bake reads the compiled creature and the
pattern stack and writes maps; nothing in compile, motion or the live materials moves for a
creature that is not exported. What the bake needs from them is opt-in: compile keeps its
distance field only when asked (decision 7), the pattern kit reads a relief threshold only the
bake sets (decision 4), and the live skin material has a coat look only the round trip asks for
(decision 16).

## The max step: what each live effect becomes

| Live effect | What glTF has | Export | Round trip renders the live side |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pattern stack (albedo, roughness) | Base colour and metallic-roughness textures | Baked per texel, at time 0 | As is, at the bake's texel size (decision 13) |
| Detail faded by pixel size (anti-aliasing) | Mipmaps | Baked at the texel's size; engines' mipmaps fade it further away | At the bake's texel size |
| Relief (`height`, a bump from screen derivatives) | Tangent-space normal texture | Baked into a normal map against the tangents written with the mesh, with glTF's handedness | As is, at the bake's texel size |
| Glow (emissive layers) | Emissive texture, and `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` above 1 | Baked at time 0, each light at its own phase, as the live creature shows at time 0; scaled into the 8-bit map by its brightest texel, which becomes the emissive strength | At time 0 |
| Glow's pulse | Nothing (no animated materials in core glTF) | Live-only: each light keeps the brightness it has at time 0. The extras list each glowing layer's `pulse` (Hz) for a game that animates `emissiveIntensity` | Not animated |
| Fur shells | Nothing usable (no shells, no fins in glTF) | Live-only. Under fur, the skin maps take the coat's mean look: its colour × 0.78 (the shells' shade, 0.55 at the root to 1 at the tip, averaged) and roughness 0.85, instead of the live skin's shadowed base. The extras keep `fur` (length, density, regions) for an engine's own fur | Without shells, the skin in the same coat look (decision 16) |
| Wrap lighting and scatter (`hide`, `skin`, fur) | Nothing equivalent (`KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission` is light through thin surfaces, not wrap) | Live-only, documented; engines show a harder terminator | With `wrap` 0 |
| Chitin's lacquer | `KHR_materials_clearcoat` | Clearcoat factor and roughness, its normal the skin's normal map (`clearcoatNormalTexture`, the same image), as three perturbs it live | As is |
| Membranes' translucency | `KHR_materials_transmission` is refraction, not backlight | The live shader's own approximation: its light-through term is emissive, so it is baked into the membrane's emissive map. Opacity goes in the albedo's alpha (`BLEND`) where any of it is see-through. Membranes are always double-sided | As is, with the live blend flags on the loaded side |
| Membrane veins (anti-aliased lines per pixel) | Base colour texture | Baked into albedo, anti-aliased at the texel's size | As is, at the bake's texel size |
| Eyes (iris, pupil per pixel) | Base colour texture | Baked per texel into their own small atlas | As is |
| Mouth inside (wet, darker to the throat) | Textures | Baked per texel, as the live shader shades it | Jaw open (decision 13) |
| Occlusion | Occlusion texture (indirect light only) | Export-only: sampled from the distance field (decision 7); the live creature has none | Off on the loaded side |
| Breathing (a swell along normals) | Morph targets | Live-only (the chest's swell is 1% of the torso; not worth a morph target per clip) | Breath 0 |

`runtime.md` lists these, and an export with textures adds the live-only ones to its `notes`.

## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Where the work lives | `@spawnforge/bake` (`packages/bake`), depending on `core`, `meshoptimizer` 1.3 (MikkTSpace-compatible tangents now, simplification in 11.2; one module instead of the plan's separate MikkTSpace build, decided here, 2026-10-08) and `watlas` 1.0.1 (xatlas as an ES module for browsers and Node, 32-bit indices; it loads its WASM with `new URL(…, import.meta.url)`, so the render page's Vite server serves it as is). No renderer and no DOM. It returns plain data (`BakedTextures` in core: typed arrays and RGBA8 images); `three`'s `buildExportScene` takes that data, so `three` does not depend on `bake`. |
| 2 | Atlases | One atlas per mesh, as each is one material: skin, parts, eyes, membranes. xatlas charts each mesh (one iteration, charts no larger than 1/40 of the mesh's area: the skin charts in about 1.5 s instead of 3) and packs it with `size / 256` texels of padding (at least 2), `rotateCharts` on. The head gets twice the texel density: xatlas sees head-weighted positions scaled up to twice about the head, and the bake uses the true positions. Vertices split at seams; every attribute is carried through xatlas's `xref`. The output must hold every non-degenerate input triangle once, in order, in one atlas. xatlas sizes its texels to fit the charts into the resolution asked; if it ever returns a second atlas, the mesh keeps vertex colours with a note (decision 11). None of the 32 examples does. High-quality skins reach 29,000 vertices; watlas takes 32-bit indices, so there is no 16-bit limit. |
| 3 | Sizes | Skin 512, 1024 or 2048 texels by quality (low, medium, high); parts and membranes half the skin's; eyes 128, 256 or 256. `export --textures <size>` sets the skin's size (the others follow), `--textures none` keeps vertex colours. |
| 4 | Evaluating a texel | Each triangle is rasterized in UV space conservatively: every texel whose centre is inside it (top-left rule, so shared edges are filled once), and then every still-empty texel within half a texel of it, with its barycentric weights clamped to the triangle, so chart borders hold real values before dilation. The weights interpolate the vertex inputs exactly as the GPU interpolates varyings live: rest position, normal (renormalized), `body` and `region` for the skin; colour and `info` for parts; `eye`, `iris` and `sclera` for eyes; colour, `info` and `vein` for membranes. The skin's stack runs once per texel through the CPU kit (`shadeSkin`, `shadeMouth`) with `pixel` the texel's size in torso lengths and `time` 0. Relief fades by a separate `Surface.reliefPixel`, which defaults to `pixel` (the live creature is unchanged) and which the bake sets to 0.4 of the texel: finite differences over two texels resolve features about 3 texels across, where a bump from 2×2 screen derivatives needs 7. Colour spaces: the stack's colours are sRGB, the mouth's and the glow linear; skin and mouth mix in linear light as live does, and albedo is encoded to sRGB once. |
| 5 | Speed | The stack costs 3–10 µs a texel; a 1024 skin covers about 680,000 texels, 2–7 s alone. The covered texels are split into bands; a band is a pure function of (compiled data, atlas, band) and returns floats (colour, roughness, height, glow). `bakeTextures` takes a `run` function that maps bands to results: in-thread by default, a pool of Web Workers in the render page and in the sandbox (one per core, at most 4, kept alive across exports, each building its registry from the packs as the compile worker does). The normal and gutter passes run after the bands are merged. |
| 6 | Normal map | The surface gradient of the height (× `scale`, metres, as live) comes from the neighbouring texels' heights and the triangle's UV-to-world Jacobian, `∇h = J (JᵀJ)⁻¹ [∂h/∂u, ∂h/∂v]`, with central differences where both neighbours are in the same chart, one-sided ones at chart edges, and a flat normal where neither is (thin charts on toes and tail tips); a neighbour is in the same chart if it was rasterized and its rest position lies within 3 texels' world size. `n' = normalize(n − ∇h)` is the live `bumpNormal`'s surface gradient. Tangents come from meshoptimizer's `generateTangents` with `Compatible` (never `ZeroFallback`), one per triangle corner: corners of one vertex that disagree split it. Their sign is negated, as three's `computeMikkTSpaceTangents` does for glTF, whose v runs down while its normal maps' +Y is up. The normal is written in that frame, solved against the interpolated tangent, bitangent (`w · n × t`) and normal rather than projected, since the frames are not orthonormal. Only the skin has relief. |
| 7 | Occlusion | `compileCreature(…, { field: true })` keeps the skin's distance field (`compiled.field`, the `Sdf` it meshed); compiles for the live creature, cached and posted from workers, leave it out. Occlusion is sampled per vertex at five distances along the normal, `dᵢ` = 0.01, 0.02, 0.04, 0.06 and 0.08 torso lengths × `scale` (the field is in metres): `1 − Σ 2⁻ⁱ max(0, dᵢ − (f(p + n dᵢ) − f(p))) / dᵢ`, clamped to 0.3–1, with head details in the field. Measured against the field's value at the point itself, so a vertex off the zero set (tubes, a horn's root inside the skin, teeth inside the head) is not darkened for that. Inside the mouth (`body.z ≤ −1`) it is 1. Interpolated per texel; skin and parts get it; eyes and membranes do not. |
| 8 | Maps per material | Skin: albedo (RGB, sRGB), normal (linear), ORM (R occlusion, G roughness, B metalness 0, linear), emissive (sRGB) only if anything glows. Parts: albedo and ORM. Eyes: albedo, with `roughnessFactor` 0.24 (their roughness is constant). Membranes: albedo (opacity in alpha only where some is see-through), ORM without occlusion, emissive (their light-through, `linear(base) · 0.22 · translucency + glow`). Metalness stays 0. One texture object is assigned to `aoMap`, `roughnessMap` and `metalnessMap`, so the exporter writes the image once. |
| 9 | Gutters | After rasterizing, each empty texel takes the average of its filled neighbours, pass after pass, until every texel within `size / 64` texels of a chart is filled (all of the padding and beyond), so bilinear filtering and the first mip levels never reach the background. The normal map's gutters are dilated as vectors and renormalized. |
| 10 | Encoding | Images leave as RGBA8 arrays; the exporter's page encodes them through a canvas: PNG for normal and ORM maps, JPEG (quality 0.92) for opaque albedo and emissive only if the medium `.glb` would pass 8 MB as PNG; never for an albedo with alpha. A canvas premultiplies alpha, so an insect membrane at 20% opacity keeps about 50 levels per channel: accepted and noted. |
| 11 | The `.glb` | Meshes get `TEXCOORD_0`, `TANGENT` (the skin) and the maps; vertex colours go. A mesh whose atlas could not be built keeps its vertex colours and adds a note: the export never fails for a texture. Node rotations are normalized before writing (today's exports fail the validator on a rotation of length 1.00000002). Extras gain `textures: { size, maps }` and, per glowing layer, its `pulse`. |
| 12 | Determinism | xatlas runs single-threaded in WASM, so the same mesh gives the same atlas, and bands merge in band order: the same creature bakes to the same images. The `.glb`'s bytes can differ, since the exporter appends images as their encodings finish, so the test compares the parsed glTF JSON and each decoded image. |
| 13 | The oracle | The headless page loads the exported `.glb` with `GLTFLoader` and renders it beside the live creature, with the loaded skeleton posed by bone name from the same `Pose`: the contact sheet's six views at rest, the head with the jaw at 0.8, and a three-quarter view with the wings spread. The live side has the round trip's simplifications: no fur shells and the coat look under fur, `wrap` 0, time 0, breath 0, and every material's `pixel` set to the bake's texel size (relief at 0.4 of it, as baked); the loaded side casts and receives shadows as live meshes do, its membranes take the live blend flags (`depthWrite` on, one pass), and its `aoMapIntensity` is 0. Views are drawn at about one pixel a texel (the head's map is twice as dense), up to 2048 pixels across, in tiles of 1024 where only the tiles the creature's bounds reach are drawn. A view first compares silhouettes (masks from an unlit render, at least 99% overlap), so pose errors are reported apart from shading; then, within the shared silhouette, both images softened by a 1-2-1 kernel each way (a texel's bandwidth: a map holds nothing finer, and the live bump from 2×2 screen derivatives sparkles at one pixel a texel, which is the live shader's aliasing, not the bake's error), the mean absolute difference must be under 3.5% of full scale and at most 5% of the pixels off by more than 20% in a channel. The `.glb` is loaded twice: with its tangents, and with `TANGENT` removed, when three builds the frame from UV derivatives the way glTF defines it; both must pass, so the tangent sign and the green channel are checked against glTF's convention rather than the bake's own. Three probe blueprints (deep scales, a matte and glossy split, bright glow) render under a grazing key light at four pixels a texel (six close up), and each effect's own contribution (the render minus the same render without that map) must correlate at 0.7 or more on average between the sides, its size within 0.7–1.4 of the live one's in every view. The oracle is mutation-tested, each mistake against the probe whose effect it breaks: the relief probe must fail with the green channel flipped, the tangent sign negated, the normal map's bytes written as sRGB, or v flipped; the roughness probe with R and G swapped in the ORM; the glow probe with glow clipped at 1. A half-texel shift is below what renders resolve; a unit test checks instead that every value sits at its texel's centre, where a GPU samples it. A unit test decodes the normal map in a frame built from UV derivatives, independent of the shipped tangents, and compares it with the normal the bake meant. The Khronos validator (`gltf-validator`, a dev dependency) must report no errors for every example's export. CI runs the round trip on four examples that cover fur, chitin, membranes and glow, at 512 with only the idle clip; `pnpm roundtrip` runs all 32 at medium and writes a report. |
| 14 | Budgets | A medium `.glb` with textures ≤ 8 MB and its export (compile, clips, atlases, textures, encoding) ≤ 10 s in the page, measured by `pnpm budgets` for every example. |
| 15 | The export eval | Gains texture tasks: export with textures at a set size and read the map sizes back; export without textures; and report which live effects the file leaves out (from `notes`). |
| 16 | The coat look | `skinMaterial` takes a `coat` option: under fur, the skin shows the coat's mean shade (× 0.78) and roughness (0.85) instead of its shadowed base. Only the round trip sets it; the bake shades fur regions the same way. |

## Done when

The round trip passes for every example, the validator reports no errors for any, the export eval
has texture tasks, a medium `.glb` stays ≤ 8 MB and exports in ≤ 10 s, and `runtime.md` lists what
each live-only effect becomes.

## Checks

`bake` unit tests: atlases hold every non-degenerate triangle once with every UV inside the map;
seams carry every attribute; texel inputs at a triangle's corners equal its vertices'; every
texel within half a texel of a triangle is filled; a flat height gives the flat normal
(0.5, 0.5, 1) and a ramp the tilt its slope asks, decoded in a frame from UV derivatives;
occlusion is 1 on a flat field and about 0.7 in a 90° crease; gutters are filled; v is 0 at row
0; the same creature twice gives the same images. The round trip, its probes and mutations, and
the validator as above.

## Results

Measured on the final code (`eval/runs/2026-10-08-phase11-textures/`):

- **Round trip:** all 32 examples pass every view with and without tangents; silhouettes overlap
  fully, the worst mean difference is 2.6% (the reed viper) and the worst share off 3.4% (the
  river crocodile); 26 of 32 stay under 1.5%.
- **Probes and mistakes:** relief correlates 0.91 (0.88–0.95 per view), roughness 0.81, glow
  1.0, each at 0.88–1.07 of the live size. The green channel flipped and the tangent sign
  negated fall to 0.55, the normal map tagged sRGB to 0.46 (and 1.3–2.0 times the size), v
  flipped to 0.26, the ORM swapped to 0.47, and clipped glow keeps its shape (0.94) at a third
  to a half of its size; the normal-map mistakes and v also fail the plain views (mean 6–11%).
- **Validator:** no errors for any example.
- **What changed on the way:** the comparison's softening, the tiles, failing an empty view, and
  freeing each shot's shadow map and loaded file (a full run had grown Chromium to 11.7 GB);
  see the run's notes.
