# Design: gaits that change with speed; run and gallop (milestone 10.1)

Plan 1's gaits are one formula. Leg `i` of pair `p`, on side `s` (0 left, 1 right), starts its
cycle at `φ = (p·w + 0.5·s) mod 1`, where `w` is the gait's `wave`. Each foot is planted for the
first `duty` of its cycle. Stride follows Alexander's dynamic similarity, `λ/h = 2.3·Fr^0.3`,
capped by what the legs reach. That covers walks, trots and the tripod gait. It cannot say:

- that a horse leads with one foreleg;
- that every foot leaves the ground at once in a run or a gallop;
- that the spine flexes and extends each stride;
- that duty falls as an animal speeds up.

10.1 adds those as data a gait module gives, so the core still names no gait. Rule one: walk,
trot, tripod and slither give the same numbers as before, so their motion tests do not move.

## What a gait module may give

- **`offsets(leg, params)`**: the phase at which a leg's cycle starts, from its pair (0 is the
  hindmost), its side and the pair count. It replaces the wave formula for that gait.
  - The gallop leads with the `lead` foreleg (`left` or `right`):
    - **transverse**: hind 0 and 0.1, fores 0.45 and 0.55, with the lead foreleg last, as a
      horse's are;
    - **rotary**: the forelegs in the opposite order to the hind legs, as a cheetah's or a
      dog's are.
  - The bound sets each pair down almost together: hind 0 and 0.04, fores 0.5 and 0.54.
- **`duty` as a profile.** A module's `duty`, or a blueprint's gait `duty` and `stride`
  parameters, may be one number or `[slowest, fastest]` across the gait's Froude range,
  interpolated linearly in Froude.
  - Plan 1's per-speed settings, which agents asked for, are this.
  - Old values are single numbers, so they mean what they meant.
- **`flex`**: how far the spine flexes and extends each stride, 0 to 1. The gallop and the
  bound use it; the rest leave it 0.
- **`hip`**: the hip heights, in metres, the gait suits. The bound is for small quadrupeds
  (≤ 0.35 m); the gallop is for the rest (≥ 0.3 m).
  - The controller leaves out the gaits whose range the creature is outside. Validation does
    not, since it does not know hip height.
- **`natural`**: the Froude number the gait looks typical at, for clips, filmstrips and
  `analyze`. The default is the old one: the middle of the range, capped at 1.

## The controller

- **Suspension.** A gait with flight is one whose feet are not always planted. That follows
  from the offsets and duty, so no module says it.
  - In each window with no foot planted, the body follows a ballistic arc. It rises by
    `g·t·(T − t)/2` over the flight time `T`, which the cycle length and the offsets give.
  - In stance, the legs absorb it.
  - The flight time is measured from the gait's own footfall pattern once per gait change, so
    the step costs nothing extra.
  - Swinging feet aim where the hip will be at landing, as now. With every foot in the air,
    nothing pins the body, so it carries on at its speed.
- **Spine flexion.** The spine bones between the girdles pitch together by
  `flex × 0.12 rad × sin(2π(φ − φ_hind))`:
  - gathered (flexed) as the hind feet land;
  - extended as the forefeet land.
  - The legs' IK takes up the change in hip position.
- **Lean.** `run` leans the torso forward by its `lean` (degrees) times the speed's share of the
  gait's range, as a sprinter leans.
- **Transitions.** When the gait changes, the controller keeps its phase. Each leg's offset then
  moves toward the new gait's along the shorter way round the cycle, at most a quarter of a
  cycle per cycle, and duty and step height blend over the same time. A planted foot is never
  lifted early by the change: legs re-arm only during stance, as now. When the creature changes
  gait at constant speed, the new pattern is reached within about two strides. Plan 1 snapped
  to the new offsets, so a walking foot could skip a step.
- **Top speed.** `maxSpeed` is the fastest gait's top. Gaits without flight stay capped at a
  Froude number of 1.5, as before, so a trotting wolf's top speed is unchanged. A gait with
  flight goes to the top of its own range.
- **Stride.** The stride cap stays: a stance may cover only the room a planted foot has. At high
  speed the creature steps faster rather than overreaching, which keeps foot slide inside the
  threshold.

## Gaits

| Gait | Legs | Froude | Duty (slow → fast) | Offsets | Flex |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `walk` | any | 0–0.5 | as before | wave | 0 |
| `trot` | 2 pairs | 0.4–2.5 | 0.5 | wave 0.5 | 0 |
| `run` | 1 pair | 0.5–8 | 0.45 → 0.3 | 0, 0.5 | 0 |
| `gallop` | 2 pairs, hip ≥ 0.3 m | 2–12 | 0.4 → 0.22 | transverse or rotary, with a lead | 0.4 |
| `bound` | 2 pairs, hip ≤ 0.35 m | 1.5–10 | 0.35 → 0.22 | pairs together | 0.6 |

With these ranges, walk gives way near 0.5, a trot or run carries on to about 2.5, and a gallop
takes over above it, as dynamic similarity predicts. A horse at 12 m/s, with a hip height of
about 1.4 m, is at Froude 10. A raptor at 8 m/s, hip about 1 m, is at 6.5.

## Analyze, clips and the runtime

- **`analyze`** runs the old walk at pace on flat and rough ground, as now. It then runs every
  other built leg gait at its natural speed, flat and rough. So `foot_slide`, `overstretch` and
  the rest are checked at a gallop too. Each check names its gait.
- **Clips** bake one cycle of every gait already, so `run`, `gallop` and `bound` bake without
  change. The runtime's distant level of detail plays the baked cycle nearest the speed.
- **Filmstrips** show every foot off the ground in a gallop's footfall diagram as a gap across
  all rows.

## Examples

- A wild horse: single hooves (`cloven: false`) on long legs. It walks, trots and gallops,
  transverse and leading right, up to 12 m/s.
- A sand cheetah: paws, a slender body and a rotary gallop. Its footfalls go round the body,
  and its back flexes.
- The rust raptor and the terror bird used to sprint as a walk with `duty` 0.4. They now leave
  their gaits to the body, so they walk, then run with both feet off the ground each stride.

Each reaches its fast gait's speed with foot slide under 2% of `scale` in `analyze`.

## Changes while building

- **Running low.** At first a gallop's stride was capped at a standing leg's room, about half a
  leg length, so a cheetah stepped 5.5 times a second. Gallopers run low, so gaits with flight
  now lower the body by 12% of the hip height, and their stance gets the room a lowered body's
  legs have. That gives the cheetah 3 strides a second, with its forelegs reaching out.
- **Postures.** A bound suited every small four-legged body, and a frilled lizard and a stone
  tortoise bounded. Gait modules now name the postures they suit: `run`, `gallop` and `bound`
  need legs under the body, so sprawlers keep their walk and trot.
- **What `analyze` checks at speed.** Every gait's run checks foot slide, overstretch and the
  ground. Limbs, heads, wings and tentacles clearing each other are judged at the walking pace,
  as before: a gallop's gathered legs brush the belly by a centimetre or two, as real ones do,
  and splaying them to clear it would spoil the walk.
