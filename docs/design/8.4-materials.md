# Design: materials, fur and new pattern layers (milestone 8.4)

As built. Where building changed the first design, the text says what was built and the
[changes](#changes-while-building) list says why.

Plan 1 has one look: a `MeshStandardNodeMaterial` whose colour, roughness and bump come from the
pattern stack (`shadeSkin`, written once against `Kit<F>`). The base materials differ only by
roughness and a little relief, scales are a 3D Voronoi mosaic, and 7.3's `hide`, `fur` and seven
pattern modules are stubs. 8.4 builds them, and tests for the first time that the GPU and CPU
backends of the kit agree.

## Today

- `shadeSkin(k, s, spec, registry)` (core `shading/compose.ts`) returns sRGB albedo, roughness and
  a height in torso lengths. Materials add a base roughness and, for scales and chitin, faint
  relief. Layers return `{ mask, color?, height?, roughness? }`.
- `skinMaterial` (three `materials.ts`) feeds those to a `MeshStandardNodeMaterial`, with bump
  from screen derivatives, the mouth's wet colours and the breathing swell.
- `bakeSkinColors` (core `export/bake.ts`) runs the same `shadeSkin` through `cpuKit` per vertex
  for `.glb` vertex colours. Nothing compares the two backends.
- `skin.fur` and `material: "hide"` validate and are reported as not built (`planned.ts`); the
  seven patterns have no `shade` hook.

## Materials

Each base material is two things: a **surface** in `shadeSkin` (relief and albedo, through the
kit, so CPU bakes get it) and a **look** in a table both backends read (`MATERIAL_LOOK` in core):
roughness, wrap, a scatter tint and clearcoat. The look's lighting terms exist only on the GPU.

| Material | Surface (kit) | Look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `skin` | Faint pores: fine value noise in the bump and ±4% in roughness | Soft: roughness 0.6, wrap 0.35 with a warm scatter tint (1, 0.66, 0.56) |
| `hide` | A wrinkle network: edges of two cell scales (0.05 and 0.018 torso lengths) cut into the bump, three times deeper in creases; wrinkles darken the albedo up to 18% | Thick and dry: roughness 0.82, wrap 0.2 |
| `scales` | Overlapping scales (below) at 0.014 torso lengths, darker toward each scale's tucked front | Roughness 0.42, wrap 0.12 |
| `chitin` | Segmented plates: seams across the body every 1/14 of the snout-to-tail axis and every quarter along a limb, plus a seam down the back; each plate domes slightly, seams darken | Hard and lacquered: roughness 0.32, clearcoat 0.7 at roughness 0.18, no wrap |

- **Wrap lighting.** `WrapLightingModel` extends Three's `PhysicalLightingModel`: its `direct`
  term adds, on top of the physical one, the light a wrapped Lambert term `(n·l + w)/(1 + w)` gives
  beyond plain `n·l`, tinted by the scatter colour. The terminator softens and warms, which reads as
  light under the skin, without a subsurface pass. `skinMaterial` overrides
  `setupLightingModel()`; chitin uses `MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial` for its clearcoat.
- **Overlapping scales** (`shingles` in core). Round scales of radius 0.85 cells on a jittered
  lattice whose rows along z are staggered by half a cell in both x and y (so the flanks and the
  back both show offset rows). Each scale is a cap that rises along the body's tail-ward
  direction, `mix(−Z, −Y, limbs)` (down the legs on limbs), from 0.25 at its front to 1 at its
  rear, and falls to nothing at its rim. A point shows the highest cap over it. So each raised
  rear edge lies over the low front of the scale behind, in scalloped rows. The relief, being
  the maximum of continuous caps, is continuous too, so bump mapping never sees a step. The low
  front of each scale darkens (by `(1 − height)²`), with no thin rim lines to alias. The `scales`
  layer uses the same shingles with its own size and gap colour. `cells` also gains the offset
  to the nearest feature and a `staggerY` option.
- **Export.** Vertex colours carry the surface's albedo, as now. Chitin's clearcoat exports as
  `KHR_materials_clearcoat` (Three's exporter writes it from `MeshPhysicalMaterial`). Wrap has no
  glTF form and stays live-only; 11.1 lists it with the other live-only effects.

## Pattern layers

Every new pattern is one module with a `shade` hook. Sizes are in torso lengths. `LayerOutput`
gains two optional fields, and `Surface` one:

- `emissive?: F`: glow strength (1 matches a lit surface), in the layer's colour, added on top of
  lighting. Bakes leave it out until 11.1's maps.
- `under?: { mask: F; color: string }`: a second colour laid before the main one (a rosette's
  centre).
- `Surface.time`: seconds, for pulses. A uniform the pose sets each frame (`Pose.time`, from the
  controller's clock); 0 in stills, bakes and the parity test, so renders stay deterministic.

| Pattern | How it is drawn |
| --- | --- |
| `scars` | Scar centres on a jittered lattice spaced `sqrt(2.5 / count)` torso lengths (2.5 square torso lengths is a typical body's skin), so about `count` lie within half a spacing of the skin along its normal; each is projected onto the skin there like a decal, a random direction laid into the skin's plane, `length` long, tapering at both ends, 0.09 × `length` wide. The 2×2×2 nearest centres are all checked, so neighbours never clip a scar. `rake` cuts repeat it at up to 0.03 torso lengths apart. Pale colour, shinier, raised along the middle and creased at the edges (`depth`). Thin bodies get the scar on both sides, like a cut round a limb. |
| `bioluminescence` | `spots`: cells, `density` of them lit, a soft core and halo; `lines`: three dashed lines along each flank (at heights −0.3, 0.15 and 0.55). Emissive at `brightness`, each spot pulsing `pulse` times a second on its own phase; the colour also shows faintly unlit. |
| `slime` | Low roughness everywhere it covers (`wetness`), a `tint`, and drips: noise stretched 8× vertically, thresholded by `drips` and stronger low on the flanks, with a bead of relief at each drip's foot. |
| `warts` | Cells with `density` kept; each a dome `(1 − t²)²` of radius `size` (varying per wart) in the bump, a shader matter as plan.md says of detail under two grid cells; rougher, coloured only toward the tip. |
| `veins` | Lines where domain-warped fbm crosses its middle (`|n − 0.5|` under a band sized from `width`), so they wander and branch; darker, raised by `raised`. Default `width` 0.01. |
| `rosettes` | Cells; a ring between 0.55 and 0.9 of `size`, broken into marks by noise in the ring (`broken`), around a centre in `centerColor` (`under`). |
| `bands` | Even rings along the snout-to-tail axis, `count` of them, `width` of each period, with edges as sharp as `sharpness`; limbs take the band where they attach. |
| `spots` | Denser by default: `density` 0.5 → 0.75 and `size` 0.05 → 0.04. Blueprints that write them keep their values. |

Pattern fades (`detail`, `relief`) take each feature's full size: a spot's diameter, a wart's
spacing, a scar's or vein's width times a few. Plan 1's spots passed 0.6 × their radius, which
faded a default spot at contact-sheet distance, where it spans 8–10 pixels and would not alias.

## Fur

`skin.fur: { length, density, region }` grows shell fur over any material.

- **One draw call.** A second `SkinnedMesh` shares the skin's geometry and skeleton, with
  `count` set to the number of shells, so the WebGPU renderer (and its WebGL 2 fallback) draws
  them as one instanced call. Its material offsets each instance along the skinned normal by
  `(i + 1)/shells × length`, with a little droop down and toward the tail at the tips.
- **Shells by quality:** none at low, 12 at medium, 16 at high. The baked level of detail
  (`setLod('baked')`) hides them.
- **Strands.** Each fragment looks up its rest-space point (the skin vertex it came from, before
  the offset) on a lattice of strands spaced `length/14`: a strand per cell (jittered, kept with
  probability `0.35 + 0.65 × density`), a random height between half and all of the length, and a
  radius of 0.72 cells at the root (so neighbouring hairs close into an undercoat) tapering to
  nothing at that height. Fragments outside the strand are discarded (`maskNode`). The tips lean
  down and back (toward the tail on the body, toward the feet on limbs).
- **Colour.** The skin's own pattern stack, evaluated in the vertex stage (`vertexStage`) with
  `pixel` fixed at the strand spacing, so stripes and spots show through and fine detail does
  not. Shells darken toward the roots (0.55 at the skin to 1 at the tips) as a cheap
  self-shadow, and use the wrap lighting with wrap 0.5 and a near-white scatter. The skin under
  fur is halved in brightness and matte, so gaps between hairs read as depth.
- **Where.** Fur length is scaled by the `region` masks (the same masks layers use), shortened
  in creases (×(1 − 0.8 crease), which includes the lip line), on the head (×0.6) and on the toes
  (to 0.3), cleared around each eye (from 1.15 to 2 eye radii, the eyes' centres and radii passed
  in as constants), and zero inside the mouth. Membranes are separate meshes (9.3), so fur never
  grows on them.
- **Export.** Shells need the instanced shader, so `.glb` files leave fur out; the export notes
  say so, and 11.1 decides fur's exported form with the other live-only effects.
- **Cost.** One extra draw call when furred. 11.3's bench measures its GPU cost; `budgets` counts
  the call.

## The parity test

Plan 1 trusted that one `Kit` function gives the same answers on both backends and never checked.
8.4 adds `packages/render/src/parity.test.ts`:

- Node compiles each test creature, picks 2,048 skin vertices (every n-th, plus mouth and crease
  vertices), and evaluates `shadeSkin` and `shadeMouth` with `cpuKit` at their surfaces.
- The render page compiles the same creature and draws one pixel per sample into a float render
  target: a quad per sample, carrying that vertex's rest position, normal, body and region as
  flat attributes, so `pixel` (screen derivative) is 0, as in bakes. The material is unlit and
  writes the stack's outputs (two passes: albedo and roughness; relief and glow).
- The test compares them: at least 99% of samples within 2/255 on every output, the rest
  reported (a sample on a cell boundary can land in a neighbouring cell after float32
  rounding).
- Creatures: two blueprints that between them hold every pattern module, each compiled with
  every material, plus the examples.

`packages/three` exports the surface-from-attributes builder and the unlit stack material it
uses, so the page and `skinMaterial` share one code path.

## Examples and checks

- Examples: the grey wolf gets fur; the tusk boar hide and claw scars; the bog troll hide and
  warts; the ember beetle (already chitin) glowing spots; the rust raptor keeps scales (now
  shingled).
- Each new pattern goes through the harness and has a render in the PR: a pattern gallery in
  `eval/runs/2026-10-06-phase8-materials/`, each layer alone on one body, and each material on
  the same body.
- Goldens unchanged (materials move no vertex); the render baselines are re-approved.
- Fur adds one draw call at most (a test counts the meshes).

## Changes while building

- **Scales.** Shingles as plates within Voronoi cells still read as a cracked mosaic: the cell
  borders dominated. Round scales where the front-most covering one wins gave scalloped rows,
  but gate 8's quality review saw black and white speckle along every rim. Bump mapping took a
  slope across the step where one scale gave way to the next, and thin dark rim lines aliased.
  The highest of continuous caps has no step, and darkening by height has no thin lines; the
  reviewers' speckle complaint was the only one that cost the new look pairs.
- **Scars.** Slabs clipped to a ball around 3D features showed almost none: few features lie
  within half a scar's length of the skin, and none on a thin serpent. Decals projected along the
  normal give about `count` on any body.
- **Fades.** Default spots, rosettes, warts and veins vanished on contact sheets; the fades now
  take the feature's full size (above).
- **Fur.** At `length/8` strands and 0.45 root radius the coat read as stubble over bare skin;
  denser strands, thicker roots and a darker, matte skin beneath read as fur. Fur first covered
  the eyes; it now keeps clear of them. The skin's red scatter showed through as pink, so fur
  scatters nearly white, and the skin's own scatter was softened from (1, 0.42, 0.32) at wrap
  0.45, which read as sunburn on the terror bird.
- **The boar** looked plush with fur, which also hid its scars; it has hide instead.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| A subsurface pass, or Three's `MeshSSSNodeMaterial` | A screen pass costs more than the look needs, and SSS there means light through thin parts; wrap is the cheap standard for soft skin |
| Scales in body coordinates (rows around the body) | `height` is the same on both flanks and carries no girth, so scales would mirror at the spine and stretch on thin tails |
| Scars placed from the compiled mesh (a compile-time hook) | Exact counts, but a new hook and per-layer data for one pattern; projected decals give "about `count`", which is what the parameter says |
| Fur colour per fragment | The full stack per shell per pixel; the vertex stage costs the stack once per vertex per shell |
| Fur as geometry (cards or strands) | Many draw calls or a large mesh, and plan 2's budget is one instanced call |
| Pulse from Three's global `time` | It runs from page load, so stills and the visual regression test would flicker |
