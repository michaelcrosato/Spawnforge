# Design: muscle masses, joints and body shape (milestone 8.1)

Every plan 1 review said the bodies read as tubes: legs of even width from hip to foot, no
chest, no hips, rods for tails. 8.1 gives every body anatomy from rules, scaled by `muscle`, so
any blueprint (and phase 9's bodies) gets it without new fields. An adversarial review of the
first draft changed most of it; see [Review](#review).

## Today

- The skin is a signed distance field (`compile/sdf.ts`): one rounded cone per skin bone (one
  per span of its radius profile when that varies within the bone), with an elliptical
  cross-section. A chain's bones join with a plain union; a chain joins its parent with a smooth
  minimum (`chain.blend`). Each limb chain has one sphere at its root (1.12 × the root radius),
  in the plain union.
- Skin weights come from each bone's cone (`skin.ts`). Parts find the surface by marching the
  field (`parts.ts`). `analyze` takes mass from the skin's volume, and limb intersections and
  ground contact from `bones.radii`; hit capsules use each bone's `max(r0, r1)`.

## Principles

1. **`s = 2 × muscle`**, 0 to 2 (default muscle 0.5 gives 1). Every change is a function of `s`
   that is exactly zero at s = 0: no masses are made, blend radii are `k × min(1, s)`, profile
   multipliers are 1, and the root sphere's blend is 0. So **`"muscle": 0` compiles to exactly
   today's mesh**, which a test checks against today's golden fingerprints.
2. **No new primitive.** Masses are rounded cones, the field's existing primitive (exact enough
   distance, existing bounds): the chest keel and the neck muscle are short capsules offset from
   the axis, with elliptical cross-sections no flatter than 2:1.
3. **Masses blend with the cones, never with each other**: a chain's value is
   `min(C, min_i smin(C, m_i, k_i))`, where `C` is the plain union of its cones. Order-free,
   bumps do not stack, and a culled mass cannot change the result.
4. **What reads radii stays as it is.** `bones.radii`, `r0`/`r1`, skin weights, toe sizes,
   junction blends and `footH` keep the blueprint's radii; profile multipliers change only the
   cones' `profile[]`. Limb tips never shrink.
5. **Measure before tuning**: primitive counts and Chrome compile times for the four examples
   are a gate before the look is tuned.

## The rules

All sizes are in the local radius `r` (the bone's radius where the mass sits, including the
section's cross-section scale) and `s`.

### Limbs (roles `leg` and `arm`; materials other than chitin)

Segment `k` of `n`, with `t` along it. Limb muscle is radial: a multiplier on the limb's radius
profile, which its bones carry on four spans each, so it is smooth and never reads as a lump.
(The reviewed draft, and rounds 1 to 3 of tuning, used offset belly capsules and knee caps as
masses; blind reviews kept calling them balloons, ball knees, club forearms and creases, and
could hardly see them at review size. See [Tuning](#tuning).)

- **Swell**: segment `k` is multiplied by 1 + A_k × s × sin(π t^0.75), which peaks 40% down the
  segment and is gone at both of its joints. A_0 (thigh, upper arm) = 0.28 + 0.22 × the share by
  which the limb is thinner than a share of the body it joins (0.42 of the torso's radius for
  legs, 0.32 for arms), so thin legs on big bodies get the heaviest thighs; A_1 (calf, forearm) =
  0.14; feet and hands 0.
- **Joints**: `1 − 0.18 s × bump(T, j/n, 0.07)` at every joint (`T` along the whole limb), and
  `1 − 0.26 s` at the last joint (ankle, wrist) when the limb has three segments or more; on two
  segments the one joint is a knee or elbow and narrows like one. Faded out by
  `1 − smoothstep(0.8, 1, T)`, so the tip never changes.
- **Stocky segments** swell and narrow less: their amplitudes are multiplied by
  1 − smoothstep(0.3, 0.55, r / ℓ) (a joint by its two segments' smaller value), so a leg as thick
  as half its length (a rhino's, a toad-like lizard's) stays a column instead of turning into
  balloons or knobs.
- **Chitin** limbs instead swell each segment: × (1 + 0.22 s × sin(π t) − 0.1 s × (1 − sin(π
  t))) within the segment (narrow at the joints, fat in the middle; the last segment's tip kept).
  The swept tubes of thin bones follow the profile, so thin legs show it too.
- The root sphere keeps plan 1's 1.12 × the root radius and blends with 0.4 r_root × min(1, s),
  so the shoulder or hip melds into the body. Growing it made balls of short legs.

### Torso (by the profile, plus one keel)

Only limbs on the torso count. Let `front` be the `at` of the frontmost torso limb pair and `hind`
of the hindmost leg pair.

- **Chest** when the torso carries arms, or two or more leg pairs at least 0.3 apart: the torso's
  radius × (1 + 0.14 s × bump(t, front + 0.08, 0.14)), and a ventral **keel** (not on chitin): a
  capsule from `chest − 0.1` to `chest + 0.14`, 0.3 s r below the axis (r scaled by the
  cross-section), reaching r (1 + 0.18 s) below it, cross-section 0.75 × the torso's, blend
  0.4 r × min(1, s): the ribcage's depth without a coincident surface.
- **Pelvis** when there is a leg pair (and the pairs do not cluster): × (1 + 0.08 s × bump(t,
  hind, 0.12)).
- **Waist** between them when they are at least 0.35 apart: × (1 − 0.09 s × bump(t, midpoint,
  0.12)).
- **Upright torsos** (pitch 45° or more: standing bipeds) get no waist and no keel, which read on
  them as a pinch above a sagging belly; their chest and hips come from the profile.
- Legs-only single pairs (a raptor without arms, the wyvern until 9.3) get a pelvis only.
  Sprawlers whose pairs cluster (hexapods and octopods, pairs within 0.3) get none of these; non
  chitin ones still get their limbs' rules.
- A torso bone without a radius profile of its own gets one point at its middle.

### Neck, tail and legless bodies

- **Neck** (bodies with a pelvis, not chitin): a dorsal capsule on the first neck bone, t 0.05 →
  0.6, offset +0.15 s r_n, radius r_n (1 + 0.15 s) − the offset, tapering to 0.85, blend 0.4 r_n
  × min(1, s): the muscle running into the shoulders.
- **Tail** (legged bodies): × (1 + 0.22 s × (1 − smoothstep(0, 0.3, t))) from the root, never
  wider than the torso's end.
- **Legless bodies** (serpents): the torso and tail sit on a flattened belly, their
  cross-section × (1 + 0.06 s) across and (1 − 0.1 s) up, and the neck narrows into a throat
  behind the head, × (1 − 0.2 min(1, s) × (1 − smoothstep(0, 0.45, at))) from the head end, so
  the head reads apart from the body instead of as the end of a tube.

### S-curved neck

`neck.curve = c` degrees makes the neck a cubic Bézier: today's quadratic (p0, p1, p2) raised
exactly to a cubic (p0, p0 + 2/3 (p1 − p0), p2 + 2/3 (p1 − p2), p2) when c = 0, with the two
middle points moved along the neck's normal by ∓ sin(c/2) × 0.35 × length (the base forward and
down, the head end back and up), the base's no lower than 0.15 × length below the neck's root so
it never dips into the chest. At c = 0 the quadratic is kept as it is, so today's necks are
unchanged bit for bit. The head and the neck's root stay where they were. Necks of fewer than 3
segments show only a C. Reach follows the new bones, as it does for any neck.

## What follows the masses

- **The field** (`sdf.ts`): masses are cones of kind 1 with their own blend (in a spare slot).
  Evaluation takes `C` per chain, then `min(C, smin(C, m, k))` per mass, then the junction fold.
  `union` (for crease depth) is each chain's value after its masses, so mass blends never read as
  creases, and crease depth is normalised by the largest junction blend.
- **Culling**: each primitive's bound radius grows by the largest mass blend of its chain, so
  blocks keep everything a mass's blend can reach; the global margin stays the junction blend.
- **Thin bones**: whether a bone is too thin for the grid is decided from the blueprint's radii
  and cross-section, before anatomy shapes them, so muscle never moves a bone between the field
  and the swept tubes. Masses on a bone dropped as thin, or with a radius under the thin
  threshold, are dropped too.
- **The grid**: its cell and lattice come from the same creature without muscle, grown by whole
  cells to hold the masses. Masses widen the field's bounds, and a grid fitted to them resamples
  everything: mouths came out jagged and thin tail tips parted where nothing had changed. Now what
  anatomy leaves alone is meshed exactly as before (a test checks the ridgeback's and the
  beetle's heads).
- **Unchanged**: skin weights, `bones.radii`, hit capsules, sockets that read radii, mass (from
  volume). A stretch test checks the skinning over a walk and a turn.

## Phase 9's bodies

Wings (9.3) are arm-like and get the arm rules; their pectoral mass is 9.3's to add on the chest
keel. Fins get the root blend only. Tentacles get the tail's muscular base. Extra necks (9.1)
each get the neck rule. Centaurs' upright human torso (9.2) is a neck section, so it needs 9.2's
own chest rule; 8.1 gives the horse body its chest.

## Checks

- **Gate before tuning**: primitive counts and Chrome compile times for the examples within
  budget (500 ms at medium, 30k triangles).
- Tests:
  - `muscle: 0` reproduces today's goldens exactly;
  - the fold is order-free and no mass changes the field outside its blend;
  - rules per role, segment and body;
  - the swell peaks inside a segment and leaves its joints alone;
  - what anatomy leaves alone (the ridgeback's and the beetle's heads) meshes exactly as before;
  - no tip shrink;
  - skin stretch over a walk and a turn within today's.
- Before-and-after sheets of every example; the 7.5 quality review of the phase 4 run's 20
  blueprints at `d101c06` against this milestone (≥ 16 prefer the new look: 3 of them are
  serpents, the riskiest); fuzz; motion tests and `analyze` with no new warnings on the examples;
  goldens re-recorded once.

## Review

An independent adversarial review read the first draft against the code and measured the
examples and the phase 4 blueprints. Its findings and what changed:

| Finding | Severity | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Masses in `bones.radii` raise new `limb_intersection` warnings (bog troll 0.008 → 0.05 L) and fail `analyze.test.ts`; capsules do not read `bones.radii` | Blocker | `bones.radii`, weights and capsules stay on the cone radii |
| `muscle: 0` adds 7–10% volume: constant mass radii and an s-independent blend; a chest coincident with the torso subtracts k/4 | Blocker | Masses only for s > 0, blends × min(1, s): muscle 0 is today's mesh exactly, tested |
| No cost analysis: 4–6 profile points and many masses double primitive counts; a larger global culling margin; the beetle is at 96% of the re-mesh budget | Blocker | 2 spans per bone; fewer masses; per-primitive culling margins; counts and Chrome timings are a gate before tuning |
| The ellipsoid approximation underestimates off-axis (0.27 at 8:1), so blends reach past culling; NaN at the centre | Major | No ellipsoid kind: capsules (exact) with cross-sections ≤ 2:1 |
| A sequential smin fold stacks bumps and depends on order | Major | `min(C, min_i smin(C, m_i))` |
| Caps sit inside the joint sphere; shoulder blades inside the torso; slim ankles do not show (full joint spheres after a narrowed segment); 2-segment feet would float | Major | Caps 0.78 r_j out at 0.34 r_j; blades dropped; narrowing symmetric on the continuous profile; tips never shrink |
| Torso masses ignore the cross-section (a hump on wide torsos) | Major | Torso shape through its own profile; the keel scales with the cross-section |
| Chest and pelvis misplace on the wyvern (legs only), the centaur (arms on the neck) and hexapods (pairs clustered) | Major | Only torso limbs; rules per body as above; centaur's human chest is 9.2's |
| Serpents, fish and chitin gain little or get worse (6 of the 20 review pairs); the tail base bulges behind a snake's torso | Major | Tail base and neck mass only for legged bodies, capped; chitin gets swollen segments; serpents a flattened belly; the risk is stated |
| Profile multipliers underspecified (r0/r1 have many readers) | Major | Only `profile[]` changes |
| Mass-aware weights capture skin across bones and fade back stripes over the shoulders | Major | Weights unchanged; a stretch test |
| Mass blends read as creases (darkening, grime) | Major | `union` after each chain's mass fold; normalised by junction blends |
| Caps pop at 15°; undefined terms; masses on thin bones; S-neck formula; centre of mass and ground clearance; arch; thigh webbing | Minor | Cap fade; terms defined above; thin masses dropped; the Bézier above; `analyze` must stay clean on the examples; torso shape follows the arch through its profile; no medial offsets |
