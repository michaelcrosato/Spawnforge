# Design: click-to-place editing (milestone 12.1)

The sandbox gets a place tab: pick a part from the catalogue, click the creature to put it there,
drag a part to move it, adjust its size and turn, undo and redo, and save. Every edit is a `patch`
of the blueprint, so the file it saves is one an LLM could have written. Core gets the inverse of
socket placement, `anchorAt`. Plan 2 rates it high (lowered): UI on existing seams, with a
round-trip oracle.

## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The inverse | `anchorAt(compiled, point, { on? })` in core: for a point in the bind pose (metres, model space), the attachment `{ on, at, angle, side }` that places a part there, with the instance it was read on (`section`, `mirror`). The section is the owner of the point's skin (its nearest skin vertex's strongest bone, `bones.owners`), or `on`'s instances. `at` is the point's nearest point on the section's centreline (a path's end bones run on past their ends, as placement extrapolates there: a head's bone stops short of the snout); `angle` is its direction around the centreline in the frame placement turns angles in, 0 to 180, and `side` the side it is on (`center` within 3° of the top or the belly). |
| 2 | Limbs | Angles run 0 to 180, and copies on a pair of limbs follow each limb, turned outward. A point on a limb's outer half anchors on the pair (`on: "foreleg"`, a copy on each limb); on its inner half, on that limb alone from the other side (`on: "foreleg.L"`, `side: "right"`), the only attachment that reaches it. |
| 3 | Bends | Inside a bend (the back of a knee) a point lies off two bones, and the ray from the nearer one can leave the skin before it reaches the point. With the distance field (`compile` with `field`), `anchorAt` tries the bones nearest first and keeps the first whose placement lands on the point; without it, the nearest. |
| 4 | What core keeps | `CompiledCreature.sections`: each body section's and limb's path as plain data (bone, `at` range, head, tail, up, radii and cross-section per bone), the centreline `buildParts` samples. The march from the centreline to the skin moved out of `buildParts` into `marchToSurface`, which `placeOnSkin(compiled, { section, at, angle, mirror })` shares, so the oracle runs the code that places parts. Nothing moves: goldens stay. |
| 5 | The oracle | For every example, every body section and limb, `at` 0.1 to 0.9 by 0.2 and `angle` 0 to 180 by 30 on both sides: a point placed on the skin that the section owns anchors back to an attachment that lands within 1 mm of it. And every surface part the examples place is built where `placeOnSkin` puts it (within 1 mm) and anchors back onto itself; frills, hoods, fins and antennae mark their middle or their own root bone, not their socket, and are left out of that check. |
| 6 | Clicking | While the tab is open the focused creature stands still in its rest pose where it was. A click raycasts the skin; the hit's barycentric coordinates on the posed triangle give the point on the bind-pose triangle, so the pose shown never matters. The sandbox compiles with the field (the worker compiler's `field` option). |
| 7 | The edit | Placing adds `{ id, type, attach }` with `applyPatch` (`add` on `parts`): the id is the type's last word and a number (`curved-1`), and the module's defaults fill the rest. Off the midline it makes a mirrored pair (`side: "both"`); on it, one part. Moving sets `attach` (`set`); removing is `remove`. Each edit validates; an invalid one is not applied and its error shows. |
| 8 | Dragging | Pressing on a part's mesh, or on the skin within 6% of a torso length of a part's root, grabs it; a ghost follows the skin under the pointer, and release sets the part's `attach` once (and recompiles, as every edit does). A drag goes wherever the skin is: a horn can move from the head to the back. A click there with nothing moved selects the part, or places a new one beside it. |
| 9 | Handles | The selected part's numeric params whose unit is a length or an angle (their `describe`: torso lengths, metres or degrees) as sliders, with its `attach.angle`; a slider writes with `set` on release. |
| 10 | Undo and redo | A stack of blueprints, one per applied edit; Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z, and buttons. Showing another creature starts a new history. |
| 11 | Saving | Save writes the blueprint to `creatures/<name>.json` through the dev server (`POST /__creatures/<name>`), which the watcher then shows like any other file. `$SPAWNFORGE_CREATURES` points the sandbox at another folder. |
| 12 | The test | `apps/sandbox/src/place.test.ts` starts the sandbox's Vite server on a temporary creatures folder and drives headless Chromium through it with real mouse events: load the grey wolf with `?place`, pick `horn.curved`, click the head, drag the horn back along the head by its root, remove it, undo, save. The saved file validates and has the horn on the head behind where it was first placed. The page says where on screen a point of the skin is (`spawnforgePlace.screenOf`), not what to do. |

## Done when

`anchorAt` round-trips on every section and limb of the examples, and a Playwright test places,
moves and removes a horn, leaving a file that validates.
