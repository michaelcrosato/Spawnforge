# Design: hit reactions and death (milestone 10.5)

Games hit creatures and kill them. 10.5 gives every creature a flinch, a stagger when a blow
would knock it over, and a death: a procedural collapse onto the game's ground, without a
physics engine, that never sinks into it. Ragdoll physics stays out of scope. The first draft
was reviewed adversarially (15 findings, 2 blockers); this is the answered design, and the
findings are at the end.

Rule one, as in 10.4: nothing changes for a creature that is neither hit nor killed.

## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What is a hit? | `hit({ direction, bone?, strength? })`: `direction` is where the blow pushes, in world space (only its horizontal part counts; a game passes the attacker-to-target vector); `bone` a bone name or index (default the torso's middle); `strength` 0 to 1 (default 0.5), 1 being a heavy blow that staggers nearly anything. |
| 2 | The flinch | A damped angular spring (2.5 Hz, ζ 0.45; slower on bigger creatures by the time scale) kicked by the blow: the spine and neck bend with the push in the body's frame, peaking near 0.35 rad × strength; a blow to the head or neck centres the bend on the neck and snaps the head too, applied after the head is levelled. It adds to whatever the body is doing. Legless bodies sway rather than bend (their trail sets their spine). |
| 3 | The stagger | The blow gives the body a velocity `(0.4 s + 0.6 s²) · √(g · hip)` (s the strength) along the push, which friction stops over 0.35 s × the time scale. If that would carry the body's middle outside its support (the planted feet's hull, each foot a disc of 0.12 hip heights) it staggers: the body slides with it and the legs step quickly until every foot has caught up; otherwise the body sways on a spring and comes back. Bipeds, whose support is a line, stagger more easily. Flyers, swimmers and legless bodies sway. |
| 4 | What is a death? | `die({ direction? })` (default: hit from its right): it ends a running action, fires `death`, and collapses over about 1.1 s × the time scale. Afterwards it is `dead`: `moveTo`, `drive`, `act`, `fly`, `land` and `hit` do nothing, and `place()` stands it up again. |
| 5 | How it falls | Legs buckle as the body sinks, and the body topples over the edge of its feet on the fall side: bodies on four or more legs, and bipeds whose torso is near horizontal (raptors, birds, wyverns), onto the side away from the blow (from straight ahead or behind: onto their right); bipeds standing upright (torso pitch ≥ 45°: the troll) along the blow, 80°; sprawlers 20° to the side, onto their bellies; legless bodies roll onto their side if their middle is taller than wide (a shark), else go limp on their trail. The fall accelerates (angle ∝ u²); a flyer's bank fades out over 0.4 s; as it comes to lie the body takes the ground's slope along and across it. |
| 6 | Not into the ground | Every bone has a support hull: the extreme points, in its own frame, of the skin, part, eye and membrane vertices it carries most, one for each of 64 directions. Each frame of a death the body is posed and lifted until its lowest point rests on the ground. Only the trunk and what it carries (thighs, arms, folded wings) lift it; legs, fins and flippers bend up off the ground bone by bone from the root (a sinking knee swings the bone above it); each head's neck droops onto the ground, found by bisection, raising the head if it must, and a head that still cannot clear the ground lifts the body; tail, tentacle and antenna springs are floored by their own hulls. The ground is sampled under each bone's lowest point, once a frame. |
| 7 | Limbs, head and tail | Feet let go of the ground and fall along a mix of the body's down and the world's down, stopping on the ground; springs lose their stiffness, feel full gravity and are damped harder, so they flop and settle. Wings fold; eyes close; the jaw falls slightly open. |
| 8 | Falling from the air or the water | A flyer killed in the air falls under gravity and collapses where it lands. A swimmer sinks to the bed, at least 0.4 m/s and fast enough to reach it in about 3 s, and settles there. |
| 9 | At rest | Decided on the fixed steps, so at any frame rate alike: once the collapse is over, it is on the floor, and nothing (its slide, its springs) moves faster than 1% of its size a second for 0.3 s, or 4 s after it reached the floor. Then `update` advances time and nothing else. |
| 10 | Clips and the runtime | A `death` clip is baked on flat ground (hit from its right, so it falls onto its left) until it rests plus 0.5 s, the root in place. The runtime's `Creature` gains `hit`, `die`, `dead` and `dying`; a dying creature stays at full detail, a dead one keeps its last pose at any distance and is never placed again by level of detail. Hit capsules follow the pose throughout. |
| 11 | Scenarios | Calls `hit` (`from`: `"left"`, `"right"`, `"front"`, `"back"` or degrees from its facing, 90 its left; `strength`; `bone`) and `die` (`from`). Events `hit`, `stagger` and `death` appear in the result; filmstrips show it. |

## Events

| Type | Fields | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hit` | `bone`, `position` (the bone hit) | A blow lands |
| `stagger` | `position` (the body) | The blow knocks it off balance and it steps to catch itself |
| `death` | `position` | It dies; the collapse begins |

## Checks

`death.test.ts`: every example dies on flat and on rough ground (`testCourse` at a quarter of
its hip height), hit from the left and from the right; no skinned vertex of its skin, parts, eyes
or membranes goes deeper than 3% of `scale` into the ground at any moment of it, and at rest it
lies on the ground (its lowest point within 3% of `scale` of it). Also: a flinch that comes back
to rest, a sway that does not stagger and a blow that does, deaths in the air and in the water,
the dead ignoring calls until placed, the same death twice and at 30 and 60 frames a second, the
death clip, and scenarios.

## Budgets

While dying, 0.2 to 0.65 ms a frame (the hydra's five heads the most) for 1.3 to 2.2 s, then
nothing; walking and flying are unchanged.

## Done when

Every example dies on flat and rough ground from hits on either side without going into the
ground by more than 3% of `scale`, with filmstrips in the PR.

## Review and answers

| # | Finding | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Blocker: level of detail revives corpses (baked idle, `toFull` places them) | A dying creature stays at full detail; a dead one keeps its pose when baked and is never placed by `toFull` (decision 10) |
| 2 | Blocker: one whole-body lift rests corpses on spikes, fins, hind wings; arms and legs unchecked | The trunk alone lifts; legs and fins settle bone by bone; heads droop on their own; springs by hulls (decision 6) |
| 3 | Springs floored by radius at joints let spikes and stingers sink | Dead springs are floored by their hulls, whole bone |
| 4 | Dying mid-leap, mid-gallop, mid-flight, in deep water | Height above the floor falls under gravity; speed slides out; a flyer's attitude fades; sinking reaches the bed in about 3 s (decision 8) |
| 5 | Stagger steps too slowly; bipeds always stagger; strength 1 undefined | Quick steps until every foot catches up; feet as discs; a gentler push; strength 1 a heavy blow (decision 3) |
| 6 | Head levelling and slither erase the flinch | The head snap is applied after levelling; legless bodies sway (decision 2) |
| 7 | Legless deaths: no roll channel; snakes float; the shark never rolls | The root turns about the ground; the body sinks to contact; taller-than-wide is judged on the middle bone's hull, not the bounds |
| 8 | Several heads; rolled bodies; the centaur's counter-pitch | Each head droops on its own about a world axis across its neck, may rise, and lifts the body if it must; dying skips the upright front's counter-pitch |
| 9 | Corpses float on slopes; pivot unstated | Topple over the feet's edge on the fall side; the ground's slope along and across is taken as it comes to lie (decision 5) |
| 10 | One ground sample per bone at its origin | The ground is sampled under each bone's lowest point |
| 11 | Cost about 3× the budget | Hull depth in the bone's frame (no rotation per point), ground cached a frame, limbs settled once a frame, droop searched from last frame; measured cost stated (Budgets) |
| 12 | Determinism and frame rate | Rest decided on steps; the droop target a function of time; the test runs at 30 and 60 frames a second |
| 13 | "Done when" weak: rough ground undefined, floating passes, living baseline | Rough ground at a quarter of the hip height; a check that it lies on the ground |
| 14 | Loose ends: size-free threshold, hits while dying, default side, limp revived tails | Threshold 1% of its size; hits ignored once dead; default from its right; limpness is a factor of the death, gone on `place` |
| 15 | Death clip one-sided, root stays | One clip, as the plan asks; the bones carry the body, so capsules follow it |
