# Design: swimming (milestone 10.3)

Plan 1 drew fish and turtles but moved them as snakes on dry ground. 10.3 adds the water
medium: a game says where the water is, and any creature with a swimming gait takes to it,
swims, dives and climbs out, with the same controller, springs and events as on land.

## The water

`update(dt, { ground, water })` takes `water(x, z)` beside the ground: `{ surface }` (its height
there) or `null` where there is none. The ground under the water is its bed. `place(x, z,
heading, ground, water)` starts a swimmer afloat. Scenarios take `"water"`: `"sea"` (deep water
everywhere) or a lake `{ x, z, radius, depth }`. `withLake` carves a lake into any ground
function as a smooth bowl, and `openSea(scale)` gives water deep enough that nothing touches
the bed. The sandbox's course has a lake.

## When it swims

A creature swims when it has a swimming gait and the water under it is deep enough: about its
hip height (0.85 × hip) for a walker, a little more than its girth (1.6 × the torso's thickest
radius) for a legless body. Shallower water it wades through on its land gaits. Crossing the
line fires a `medium` event and changes gait (with a `gait` event); climbing out, every foot
finds the ground under its hip again. A body that only swims turns back rather than beach itself:
it stops where the next step would be too shallow.

## Gaits

Speeds follow Froude numbers as on land, of the hip height or, for a legless body, of 0.12 of its
length, so a 2.4 m shark cruises at 1 m/s and a 1 m turtle at 0.8. Which gaits a body gets
follows from its limbs (`motionData`):

- `swim.undulate`: a wave down the body into the tail. Fish, snakes, crocodiles at speed. A body
  with flippers and a short tail does without it.
- `swim.paddle`: legs cycling in circles under the hips, at the surface, slowly.
- `swim.flap`: long fins (length 0.4 or more) beat as flippers. Short fins only steer.

Its pace in the water is its slowest swimming gait's natural speed, so `moveTo` without a speed
walks at its walking pace and swims at its swimming pace, switching as it crosses the shore.

## Holding a height

Paddlers, and walkers not asked to dive, float with the back awash (0.4 girth under the
surface), heads out. Divers (fish, and anything given a height) hold their depth, or go to the
height `moveTo` asks for (`{ x, y, z }`), between 1.3 girths under the surface and 1.3 girths
(plus 0.6 hip heights with legs) over the bed. They climb or dive at up to about 35° to their
travel, easing out over the last stretch, and pitch toward it. A pitched body dips its snout (or
its tail) below its middle, so the floor rises by that much: a long-snouted crocodile levels out
before its nose reaches the bed.

## The beat

The tail beats at a frequency from a Strouhal number of 0.3: f = 0.3 · U / A, with U the speed
and A the tail tip's side-to-side sweep, the gait's `amplitude` (0.18 by default) times the
body's length; at rest it keeps a slow beat, slower on bigger creatures. The tail swings at its
root far enough for its tip to cover the sweep (a third as far when legs or fins drive), and a
wave runs down the body into it. A sprawler's walking wave stops in the water. Flippers beat
with the same phase, legs paddle in circles or trail back along the body, and the body rolls
into turns.

## Checks

`analyze` runs a body that only swims in open water instead of on the ground, and for every
swimmer:

- **At the surface**, in open water at its swimming pace: a creature that swims at the surface
  (paddlers and walkers) should hold its head out. `head_underwater` warns when the top of the
  head averages under the surface, with the neck and head pitch as the fix.
- **Diving** at the bed of water a few body depths deep: `hits_bed` warns when any part of the
  body goes into it.

`speed.swim` gives its top speed in the water; descriptions say "It swims at about 1.0 m/s" or
"… and swims up to 1.6 m/s"; `rpg` gains `swim`.

## Clips and the runtime

Swimming cycles are baked in open water with the surface at y = 0, so the root's height in the
clip is its depth. The runtime takes `water` in `update` and `spawn`; at a distance, on baked
cycles, a swimmer keeps its depth and plays its swimming cycle.

## Done when

Shark-like (`reef-shark`), turtle (`sea-turtle`), crocodile-like (`river-crocodile`) and
serpent (`reed-viper`, `kraken`) examples swim, dive and climb out on the course, `analyze`
checks swimming, and motion costs ≤ 0.15 ms per creature. `swimming.test.ts` checks the
crossing, the dive, the beat against speed, the turtle at the shore, the checks, scenarios,
stats and baked cycles.

## Changes while building

- Plan 1's snakes used 0.12 of their length as their speed scale. Swimming keeps it for every
  legless swimmer, and the hip height for walkers, rather than a separate scale in water, so a
  snake slithers and swims on one scale. A shark had cruised at 0.36 m/s only because its
  filmstrip and `moveTo` used the walking pace; the pace now follows the medium.
- A swish of a fixed 0.55 rad swung a crocodile's long tail almost sideways, and the sprawler's
  walking wave added to it; the swing now comes from the sweep, and the walking wave stops in
  the water.
- A diving crocodile put its snout 48 cm into the bed: the floor now rises with the pitch.
- Leaving the water switched to walking without a `gait` event; it fires one now.
