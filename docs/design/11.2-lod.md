# Design: levels of detail (milestone 11.2)

Live creatures draw every triangle at any distance, and exports carry one mesh per material.
11.2 simplifies the skin and the hard parts into a chain (100, 50, 25 and 10% of the triangles)
with meshoptimizer, swaps the levels live by screen size, and writes them into exports. The plan
rates it high (lowered): a mature library behind a clear interface, checked by triangle counts
and simplification error. There is no max step.

Rule one, as in 11.1: a creature drawn at full detail does not change. Levels only ever drop
triangles; vertices, weights, normals, body coordinates and UVs stay as they are.

## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Where | `@spawnforge/bake/lod`, a second entry point of the bake package, so it loads meshoptimizer's simplifier and nothing of the atlas: `simplifyChain(mesh)` returns each level's index buffer over the mesh's own vertices, its triangle count and its error. The types (`LodLevel`, `LodChain`) and the choice of a level by screen size (`pickLevel`) are plain data and arithmetic in core. |
| 2 | Simplifier | `MeshoptSimplifier.simplify` from the full mesh for each level (not cascaded, so each level's error is measured against the full mesh), with `ErrorAbsolute` so the error is in metres, a target error of the mesh's size so the triangle count rules, and the bind pose's positions. Measured on the examples at medium: every level hits its count exactly, 2–10 ms a level; the beetle's skin (27,340 triangles) errs 0.20, 0.57 and 1.42 mm at 50, 25 and 10%, the wolf's parts 0.27, 0.89 and 2.55 mm. `Regularize` triples the error for no visible gain, so it is off; `Prune` changes nothing on these meshes and is off. |
| 3 | Seams and borders | Vertices that share a position are seams to meshoptimizer, which keeps them closed: the UV seams of a textured export, and the mouth's split rim. Open borders (the lips, the eye sockets) collapse only along themselves. |
| 4 | Which meshes | Skin and parts, as the plan says. Eyes are under 1% of a creature's triangles; membranes are a few thousand triangles whose rows carry the wing's stations; both keep their one level. Fur shells share the skin's geometry, so they draw its level. |
| 5 | Choosing a level | The coarsest level whose error projects to under one pixel: `error · pixels / (2 · distance · tan(fov / 2))` for a perspective camera, `error · pixels · zoom / (top − bottom)` for an orthographic one, `pixels` being the viewport's height (default 1080). `distance` is from the camera to the creature's root, less its bounding radius, so a long creature's near end decides. |
| 6 | Live | `createBestiary` computes a compiled creature's chain the first time one of its creatures is given a camera (`update(dt, { camera, pixels })`), by loading `@spawnforge/bake/lod`; the chain is cached with the compiled creature, so a herd of one species simplifies once. Each frame a creature takes the level its distance allows by setting its geometry's index (the level's index attributes are shared by every creature of the species); nothing else changes. `Creature.detail` reads the level (0 is full); `lods: false` turns it off. It sits beside the baked-motion level of detail, which keeps its own distance. |
| 7 | Exports | Nodes `skin_LOD1`, `skin_LOD2`, `skin_LOD3` (and `parts_LOD…`), the naming Unity uses for LOD groups, as skinned meshes on the full mesh's skeleton that share its vertex accessors (the exporter writes shared attributes once): only the indices differ. They are attached to the full mesh's node with `MSFT_lod` (its `ids`, and `MSFT_screencoverage` in its extras for each level's switch at 1080 pixels), and are not in the scene's tree, so loaders and viewers without the extension show only the full mesh rather than four on top of each other. The extras list the chain: `lods: { skin: [{ triangles, error }], parts: […] }`, error in metres. `export --lods none` leaves the chain out. |
| 8 | Textured exports | The chain is simplified from the atlas mesh (split at UV seams), so the seams stay closed and every texel stays where it was baked. |
| 9 | Budgets | The chain of a medium creature in ≤ 100 ms; an export grows by its index buffers, about 0.85 × the full mesh's. |

## Done when

Tests show each level within 5% of its target triangle count, errors rising level by level, the
level the runtime picks projecting its error under one pixel and the next finer one's not
forcing a switch early, and every example's export with its chain passing the validator.

## Checks

`bake` unit tests: counts per level, rising errors, indices inside the mesh, seams kept closed
(a seam's two vertices are used together or not at all), the same chain twice. Core:
`pickLevel` against hand-computed distances for both cameras. Three: a bestiary creature moved
away from the camera steps down through the levels and back. Render: every example exported with
its chain validates, its LOD nodes are named, listed by `MSFT_lod` and left out of the scene.
