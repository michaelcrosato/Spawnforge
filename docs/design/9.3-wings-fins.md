# Design: wings, fins and membranes (milestone 9.3)

7.3 put wings, fins and their surfaces in the format: limb roles `wing` and `fin`, a `membrane`
slot on limbs holding `membrane.bat`, `membrane.insect`, `membrane.feather`, `membrane.case` or
`membrane.fin`, and the surface parts `fin.dorsal` and `fin.tail`. All of them are stubs:
`buildable` drops wing and fin limbs, and `notBuilt` reports them. 9.3 builds them. Flight (10.4)
animates what 9.3 builds, so the skeleton, the poses and the skinning chosen here are what flight
inherits. That is why this milestone is max.

Rule one: creatures without wings or fins compile to exactly today's mesh and fingerprints.

## Today

- Limbs are chains posed by the coupled IK: legs reach the ground, arms hang (`lift` raises them).
  Their bones are in the SDF, and bones thinner than about a grid cell become swept tubes.
- A foot module grows toe chains off the limb's tip (`hooks.toes`). The compiler appends the toes
  as bones, and the foot's `build` gets them as sockets (`ctx.toes`).
- Hard parts go into one mesh (`parts`), with a colour, a material and `t` per vertex. They are
  bound to the socket's skin weights or rigidly to a bone (`emit(…, { bone })`).
- Three skinned meshes share one skeleton, one draw call each: skin, parts and eyes. Fur adds an
  instanced fourth.
- The rig has `wings`, `fins` and `tentacles` lists (`LimbChainRig`: id, side, bones), always
  empty. Driven chains (`rig.chains`) carry named poses as turns about each bone's local X (the
  eyelids' `closed`).
- Bounds come from the bind pose. The render page frames the creature by them and poses it with
  `applyFace` (jaw, blink).

## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Bind pose of a wing | **Spread.** The skeleton builds wings spread, and the membrane is built flat in that pose. Folded is a pose applied on top, the default everywhere (controller, render, clips). | A membrane is a sheet between bones: it can only be built where the sheet is flat. Folding by skinning crumples it the way a real wing folds, between converging fingers. A glTF bind pose with wings out is the usual T-pose. |
| 2 | Wing bones in the skin mesh | **Swept tubes, never in the SDF.** Wing and fin bones are flagged `tube`: left out of the field and of the grid's extent, and drawn as tapered swept tubes (as toes are). The shoulder joins the body through a mass on the torso. | Spread wings would multiply the grid's extent: a dragon's 7 m span would mesh its 2 m body three times coarser. Tubes keep the body exactly as it was. |
| 3 | Poses | A `WingRig` holds the arm bones, the digit chains and a `folded` pose: one **local rotation quaternion per bone**, applied instead of the bind rotation, blended by slerp. Spread is the bind pose. | Folding turns joints about several axes (the shoulder swings back and down, the elbow and wrist fold in the wing's plane), and the eyelids' one-axis convention cannot say that. Quaternions are plain data and slerp cleanly. |
| 4 | How the fold is computed | At compile time, by a **Z fold with clearance**: the humerus points back and down, the forearm forward and up, the hand and digits back along the flank, each bone turned outward in 5° steps until its far end clears the body (the bind SDF) by its own radius plus 2% of the torso length. | Deterministic, any body shape, and folded wings stay clear of the body by construction. The SDF is already built; the body does not move when only the wings fold. |
| 5 | Where membranes are drawn | **One new mesh, `membranes`**, skinned, double-sided, with colour, opacity, translucency and a vein coordinate per vertex: one draw call for every membrane, feather and fin on the creature, as the budget allows. Wing cases are hard chitin and go in `parts`. | Budgets: three draw calls plus one with fur and one with membranes. Membranes need what parts lack: no culling, light through from behind, see-through insect wings. |
| 6 | See-through | Bat, feather and fin membranes are opaque and let light through from behind (translucency). Insect wings are also see-through: opacity per vertex, blended in the transparent pass with depth writes on. | One material, no sorting inside the mesh. With depth writes on, an insect wing seen through another may hide it; with depth writes off, the opaque bat membrane in the same mesh would draw out of order. The first is the smaller harm. |
| 7 | Membrane skinning | Each membrane vertex lies between two **spars** (bone polylines: an arm, a digit, the body's side, a leg) at a fraction across. Its weights blend the two spars' weights at that point (each spar point rigid to its bone, blended at joints), keeping the four largest. | It moves with the bones it spans, as plan 2 asks. Folding converges the spars, so the sheet between them gathers into pleats. |
| 8 | Bounds | `bounds` is the **folded** pose's (CPU-skinned once at compile), which is what renders and `analyze` show. `spreadBounds` is the bind pose's. `analyze` adds `measurements.wingspan`. | Framing, size lines and the plausibility checks should see what stands there. |
| 9 | What folds and spreads | A new action goal, `wings` (0 folded, 1 spread), damped in the controller. `roar` spreads them (0.8); 9.5's `display` will too, and 10.4's flight drives them. The render request takes `pose.spread` (`render --pose spread`). | Flight later needs exactly this channel. |
| 10 | Fins | Fin limbs are short chains with a flat cross-section (tubes with a flat profile), posed out and back from the body. Their membrane (`membrane.fin`) spans rays fanned from the fin's root. A flipper (`membrane: null`) is the flat limb alone. `fin.dorsal` and `fin.tail` are surface parts emitting plates into the membrane mesh. Fins have no folded pose. | As the format says. A fish's fins stay out, and 10.3 beats them. |

## Wing skeleton

- **Arm.** It roots where the limb attaches, as arms do, and the spread bind pose points out from
  the body:
  - along the outward direction, raised 10° and swept back 10°;
  - the elbow bent 15° forward and the wrist 10° back, a shallow M as wings are held in flight.
  - Segment shares are humerus 0.32, forearm 0.45 and hand 0.23 for three segments, and 0.45
    and 0.55 for two.
  - Radii follow `radius`, and the wing gets the arm muscle rule (8.1: the humerus fills out),
    drawn on its tube.
  - Bone names follow limbs: `wing.L.0…`.
- **Pectoral mass.** The wing's shoulder is a mass on the torso chain, so it is part of the body
  mesh and the tube sinks into it:
  - a sphere of 1.3 × the root radius at the limb root;
  - with muscle, a flight muscle (`0.25 s` of the torso radius) along the chest under the root.
  - This is the "pectoral mass" 8.1 left to 9.3.
- **Digits** come from the membrane module through the toe hook, renamed `digits` and kept as an
  alias for feet. The hook gets a `DigitContext`: the wrist, the arm's direction, the wing's
  plane (its normal), the limb length, the tip radius and the mirror.
  - `membrane.bat`: `fingers` digits fan from the wrist through 100° in the wing's plane, from
    along the arm (the leading edge) to back toward the body. They are 3 bones each, of length
    `span` × arm length × (1, 0.95, 0.85, 0.75, 0.65), radius 0.45 → 0.15 of the arm's tip.
  - `membrane.feather` grows one short digit (the alula's bone, length 0.25 × the hand).
  - `membrane.insect` and `membrane.case` grow none: their wing limb is the leading vein or the
    case's hinge, 1 or 2 segments.
  - Digit bones are tubes, section `digit`, named `wing.L.d0.0…`.
- **The wrist claw.** A `foot` on a wing grows its toes as today, a thumb with a claw. `foot.claw`
  on a wing makes one short toe pointing forward along the leading edge.

`WingRig extends LimbChainRig { digits: number[][]; folded: number[] }`. `folded` holds 4 numbers
per bone, over `bones` and then the digits in order. Fins use `LimbChainRig` as is.

## The fold (decision 4)

Built in model space, then converted to local rotations. Let F be forward, U up, and O outward on
the wing's side (horizontal). For each bone in turn, from the shoulder:

- **Bat and dragon** (`membrane.bat`, and wings with no membrane):
  - the humerus aims back and down, `-F·0.75 − U·0.35 + O·0.3`;
  - the forearm aims forward and up, `F·0.85 + U·0.25 + O·0.15`;
  - the hand and every digit aim back, `-F·0.9 + U·0.1 + O·0.2`. The digits fan downward by 6°
    each, like a closed umbrella's ribs.
- **Feathered:** the same Z, with the hand back and slightly up.
- **Insect** (`membrane.insect`): the wing lies back over the abdomen, `-F + U·0.15 + O·0.12`.
  - A wing behind a case on the same side sinks 3% of the torso length lower, so its case
    covers it.
- **Case** (`membrane.case`): back along the top of the abdomen, `-F + O·0.05`. Its outer edge
  meets its pair along the midline.

Clearance: if a bone's far end (or its middle) lies within its radius plus `0.02 L` of the body's
bind SDF (torso, neck, tail and legs; not wings), the bone turns 5° about the axis that moves that
end outward, up to 18 steps. Each bone's local quaternion is then its folded world rotation
relative to its parent's folded world rotation, and its twist is kept by aiming from the bind
direction (`Pose.aim`'s swing). Pure numbers, computed once; folded wings clear the body at rest
by construction, and a test checks it.

## Membranes

Every membrane module builds in the spread (bind) pose with a new context call,
`ctx.membrane(sheet, options)`.

- **`sheet`**: rows of points across spars, each row holding its bone weights. Helpers build
  sheets from two polylines (`between(a, b, rows, cols, edge)`), so modules never handle
  weights. Spars are polylines of a limb's, a digit's, the body's or a leg's points; the context
  provides `ctx.spars`:
  - `arm`;
  - `digits[i]`;
  - `flank`: along the body's side, from the wing root back to the hip of the nearest
    same-side leg behind;
  - `leg`: down that leg to the ankle, or none.
- **`membrane.bat`:**
  - **panels between consecutive digits**, wrist to tips, with the trailing edge between two
    tips dipping by `scallop` × the gap;
  - **the inner panel**, from the arm and last digit to the flank and leg (or to the flank alone
    when `trailing` is `body`, or when there is no leg behind), its trailing edge from the last
    tip to the ankle, also scalloped;
  - **a leading strip** (propatagium), 12% of the forearm's length in front of the arm, from
    the shoulder to the wrist.
  - Rows every 2–4% of span, by quality.
  - Opacity 1, translucency from the parameter, veins 0.
- **`membrane.insect`:** a plate in the wing's plane, from the outline by `shape` (narrow, broad,
  round), `width` × length wide, rigid to the wing bones along its length.
  - The vein coordinate runs from the root.
  - Opacity `1 − 0.8 × translucency`, veins `veins`.
- **`membrane.feather`:** feather cards, each a leaf outline (tapered, with a rachis line in the
  vein channel), rigid to its bone. There are three ranks:
  - **primaries** fanned from the hand past the tip, `tips` shaping the last few (rounded,
    pointed, or fingered, with the outer five spread apart);
  - **secondaries** along the forearm pointing back, each overlapping the next by half;
  - **coverts**: two short rows over the bones.
  - `color` runs to `tipColor` along each card. `feathers` sets primaries + secondaries.
- **`membrane.case`:** a domed shell (a displaced plate, `dome` high) over the wing bone, as long
  as the limb and as wide as the torso radius there, with `ridges` along it. It is emitted into
  `parts` (chitin, `sheen` as roughness) and is rigid to the case's bone.
- **`membrane.fin`:** `rays` rays fanned from the fin limb's root through 70° behind it, as long
  as the limb, with skin between them and a scalloped edge. The rays are in the vein channel.
- **`fin.dorsal`, `fin.tail`:** plates (`plate` in the kit, thickness 1.5% of their size) from
  their outline, emitted with `membrane: { translucency: 0.15 }` on the socket's weights. The tail
  fin's are those of the bones near the tip.
  - `forked`, `crescent` and `rounded` stand vertically.
  - `flukes` lie flat.

The membrane mesh carries positions, normals, indices, skin weights (4), and a colour, an
`info` (opacity, translucency) and a `vein` (strength, coordinate) per vertex. A piece emitted
with `{ membrane: { translucency, opacity?, veins? } }` from any part goes there, so fins and
future frills (9.5) use it too.

## Material

`membraneMaterial()` in `packages/three`:

- double-sided, flat normals flipped toward the eye on back faces;
- the base colour, darkened along veins (`vein.x` × a thin line pattern on `vein.y`);
- back-lighting: `translucency × colour × max(0, −N·L)` with a warm tint, so light shows
  through.
- When any vertex has opacity below 1, the material is transparent with depth writes on.
  Roughness 0.6 (feathers 0.75).

The export writes membranes as their own primitive: double-sided, vertex colours,
`alphaMode: BLEND` when see-through. The vertex-colour bake (6.1) bakes vein darkening into the
colours.

## Motion

- **The `wings` goal.** The controller blends each wing bone from its bind rotation toward
  `folded` by `1 − spread`, every step after the body is posed and before springs.
  - `spread` is damped toward the goal (default 0) at a rate that takes about 0.4 s × timeScale.
  - At rest and walking, wings stay folded and breathe with the body (a 2° lift at the shoulder
    with `breath`).
  - `roar` sets `wings: 0.8` while it holds.
- **Fins** stay at their bind pose, with a small sway on sprawlers' wave.
- **Clips and export** bake the folded wings through the controller as they do everything else.
- **`applyRest(pose, rig, { jaw, blink, spread })`** replaces `applyFace` (which stays as an
  alias). The render page poses wings by `request.pose.spread` (default 0, folded), and frames
  `bounds` or `spreadBounds` by it.
- **`render --pose spread`**, and `rest` (the default), in the CLI and the MCP `render` tool.

## Analysis

- **`wing_intersection`**, in the walk check beside `limb_intersection`. Wing and fin bones, and
  every 8th membrane vertex skinned by the same pose, are tested against the body's bone
  capsules (torso, neck, tail, legs) frame by frame. Its own shoulder's first 30% is skipped.
  - The deepest pass over 1 cm warns, naming the wing.
  - The fix advises attaching it higher (`attach.angle` lower) or further forward, a shorter
    `length` or `span`, or `trailing: "body"` when the membrane meets the leg.
- The **static check** at rest (folded) uses the SDF directly: any membrane vertex inside the
  body warns the same way.
- **`measurements.wingspan`** comes from the spread bounds when there are wings.
- The description names wings and fins: "leathery wings", "feathered wings", "fins" (the
  modules' `describe`).

## Body plans and examples

- **`wyvern`**: as preset, with `membrane.bat` on the wing forelimbs, `trailing: "leg"` and 4
  fingers.
- **`fish`**: legless, with:
  - `fin.tail` (forked) and `fin.dorsal`;
  - two pectoral fin limbs (`at` 0.2, `angle` 120, `length` 0.3) and two pelvic ones (`at`
    0.55, `length` 0.18);
  - `media` water;
  - on land it rests on its belly as a serpent does, until 10.3 makes it swim.
- **Examples** (`examples/`):
  - `ash-dragon` (quadruped with wings at `angle` 35 on the back, `membrane.bat`, trailing
    `body`);
  - `cave-bat` (the wyvern, small, with 5 fingers, big ears and a short tail);
  - `storm-wyvern`;
  - `rhino-beetle` (hexapod with `membrane.case` and `membrane.insect` pairs);
  - `luna-moth` (hexapod with broad insect wings, fore and hind pairs; antennae come in 9.4);
  - `reef-shark` (fish).
  - Each gets its render. The new tests check that it renders folded and spread, walks (the
    shark slithers) without `wing_intersection`, and stays within budgets.

## Budgets

- Membranes add one draw call (5 with fur).
- Wing cases count toward hard parts (≤ 20k triangles at medium).
- Membranes are capped at 12k triangles at medium: rows and feather counts scale down to fit.
- Compile time stays ≤ 500 ms per example at medium. The fold costs a few hundred SDF samples;
  the membranes are a few thousand vertices.
- Motion per creature stays ≤ 0.1 ms walking: one slerp per wing bone per step.

## Tests

- **Unchanged:** every golden and render baseline of creatures without wings or fins.
- **Skeleton:** wing bones are tubes, outside the grid's extent (the body's cell size equals the
  same creature's without wings), named as limbs; digits per membrane module; the fold is
  deterministic.
- **Fold:**
  - every folded wing bone and every membrane vertex lies outside the body's SDF;
  - the folded wing's span is under 40% of the spread one;
  - insect wings fold over the abdomen, under their cases.
- **Membranes:**
  - the vertex count scales with quality;
  - weights sum to 1 over at most four bones;
  - every membrane vertex moves with the bones it spans (folding moves it, and moving the body
    alone moves it as the body);
  - see-through only for insect wings.
- **Motion:**
  - the `wings` goal spreads and folds over about 0.4 s;
  - `roar` spreads them;
  - walking keeps them folded;
  - no `wing_intersection` on any example over the walk check.
- **Render:** `--pose spread` widens the frame to the wingspan; visual regression for every new
  example.
- **Harness:** every built membrane module and fin part goes through the module harness:
  defaults, its example, time and size budgets, and the same twice.

## Not in 9.3

- Flight, gliding and hovering (10.4); swimming and fin beats (10.3).
- Body feathers and fur on wings: wings take no fur.
- A bat preset (Later).
- Wing membranes torn or scarred (Later).
- Membrane patterns beyond veins and colour (the `wings` layer region stays reserved, as in
  8.4).
