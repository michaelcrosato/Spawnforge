# Design: wings, fins and membranes (milestone 9.3)

7.3 put wings, fins and their surfaces in the format:

- limb roles `wing` and `fin`;
- a `membrane` slot on limbs, holding `membrane.bat`, `membrane.insect`, `membrane.feather`,
  `membrane.case` or `membrane.fin`;
- the surface parts `fin.dorsal` and `fin.tail`.

All of them are stubs: `buildable` drops wing and fin limbs, and `notBuilt` reports them. 9.3
builds them. Flight (10.4) animates what 9.3 builds, so the skeleton, the poses and the skinning
chosen here are what flight inherits. That is why this milestone is max.

The first draft was reviewed adversarially. Its 22 findings and the answers are at the end, and
the design below is the answered one.

Rule one: creatures without wings or fins compile to exactly today's mesh and fingerprints.

## Today

- Limbs are chains posed by the coupled IK: legs reach the ground, arms hang. Their bones are in
  the SDF; bones thinner than about a grid cell become 7-sided swept tubes.
- A foot module grows toe chains off the limb's tip (`hooks.toes`), which the compiler appends
  as bones.
- Hard parts go into one mesh (`parts`), bound to a socket's skin weights or rigidly to a bone.
  Three skinned meshes share one skeleton, one draw call each; fur adds an instanced fourth.
- The rig has empty `wings`, `fins` and `tentacles` lists. Driven chains carry named poses as
  turns about each bone's local X (the eyelids' `closed`).
- The bind pose is also the rest pose everywhere:
  - `Pose` starts from it, and the Three.js bones and glTF nodes default to it;
  - bounds and markers come from it;
  - an exported clip writes a track only for bones that change within that clip.

## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Bind pose of a wing | **Spread**, with the membrane built in it. Folding is a pose. |
| 2 | Rest versus bind | **Separate.** `BonesData.rest` (local rotations, present only when some bone's rest differs from its bind) holds the folded wings. Everything that shows or animates a creature starts from rest: `Pose`, the Three.js bones, glTF node defaults, bounds, markers and labels. Inverse bind matrices stay at the bind pose, so goldens of creatures without wings stay put. Clips write a track for every bone that differs from its node default. |
| 3 | Wing bones in the skin mesh | **Swept tubes, never in the SDF or the grid's extent**, with sides by radius over cell size (7 to 16) and the limb helper bones at the elbow and wrist. The shoulder is a mass on the torso where the humerus leaves the skin. |
| 4 | The fold | **Joint space.** One 3D turn at the shoulder in the root's own frame, then turns about the wing-plane normal only at the elbow, wrist and digit joints, with the digits flexing in a Z. The numbers come from the membrane module (`hooks.wing`), so the core names no module. Clearance is computed at compile time against a quality-independent field of the body (bones and masses at any radius, no wings), the arms and the ground, and adjusts the shoulder's angles. Stored as named poses (`folded`; 10.4 adds `flexed`). |
| 5 | Membrane skinning | **Station bones.** Each panel between two spars gets stations along it. A station is two bones, one on each spar's bone, aimed at each other with a shared roll. A vertex is weighted bilinearly to two stations × two ends, four weights, and lands exactly on the ruled surface between the spars at every station, whatever the spars do (folding, flapping, a swinging leg). `applyStations` poses them after limbs, wings and springs, in the controller and the render page alike, and clips bake them. |
| 6 | Feathers | **Bones per feather group** (3 or 4 cards each), with folded rotations computed so they stack back along the flank. Ranks are offset along the wing's normal so cards never z-fight. |
| 7 | Where membranes are drawn | **One new skinned mesh, `membranes`**, double-sided, one draw call for every membrane, feather card and fin. Wing cases are hard chitin and go in `parts`. |
| 8 | Membrane colour | **The pattern stack runs on membranes.** `Surface` gains `wings` (1 on membranes and on wing tubes, 0 elsewhere; region weights still sum to 1), and the `wings` layer region masks by it. A membrane's base colour is its module's, and layers whose region is `wings` draw on it, which makes the region 7.3 reserved live. Veins are a channel the shader draws as lines. Fur skips wings. |
| 9 | See-through | Opacity per vertex. Insect wings are see-through; bat, feather and fin membranes are opaque and lit through from behind (translucency). The material is transparent (single pass, depth writes on) only when some vertex is see-through. |
| 10 | Fins | Fin limbs are tubes with a flat cross-section, the flat side set by an `up` option new to `sweep`. Their membrane spans rays fanned from the root. A flipper is the flat limb alone. `fin.dorsal` and `fin.tail` are built row by row with interior vertices and emitted into the membrane mesh. Fins do not fold. |
| 11 | The wings goal | `wings` (0 folded, 1 spread) blends rest toward bind. It is damped, and `roar` sets 0.8. It is a fold channel, not flight: 10.4 adds stroke and flex beside it, from the poses and the frame recorded here. |

## Rest and bind (decision 2)

- **`BonesData.rest?: Float32Array`**: four numbers per bone, the local rotation at rest.
  - It is absent when rest equals bind, so nothing without wings changes, and fingerprints do
    not hash it then.
  - `Pose` builds `restRot` from it (falling back to the bind locals) and `restWorld*` by
    forward kinematics.
  - New `bindRot` and `bindWorld*` keep the bind for whoever needs it.
- **`bounds`** are the rest pose's, from the skin, parts and membranes CPU-skinned once at compile
  time when `rest` is present. `spreadBounds` holds the bind's.
- **Markers** gain `bone`, and the render page poses them through the skeleton, so labels follow
  folded and spread wings. Wings get limb markers and a `tip.<wing id>` socket.
- **Three.js and export.**
  - `createCreatureObject` and `buildExportScene` bind at the bind pose, then set every bone to
    rest.
  - `clipOf` writes a track when a bone differs from its rest anywhere in the clip.
  - A glb round-trip test checks that a winged example's nodes default to the folded wing.

## Wing skeleton

- **Root and bind direction.**
  - Wing roots sit at 0.85 of the section's radius, so the humerus leaves the skin at once.
  - The bind direction blends the root's surface normal with the outward side (60% outward), so
    a dragon's back-mounted wing (angle 35) rises off the back instead of running through it.
  - The module's `bind` bends shape it in the wing plane (spanned by that direction and forward):
    a bat or bird wing gets a shallow M (elbow 15° forward, wrist 10° back); insect wings and
    cases are straight.
  - Segment shares are 0.32 / 0.45 / 0.23 for three segments, 0.45 / 0.55 for two, and 0.28 /
    0.38 / 0.2 / 0.14 for four.
- **Pectoral mass.** A sphere of 1.3 × the root radius where the humerus leaves the skin. With
  muscle, a flight muscle of 0.25 s of the torso radius runs along the chest under it. Wings are
  left out of `torsoPlan`, since this mass is their chest.
- **Digits** come from the membrane's `digits` hook. It is the toe hook generalised; feet keep
  `toes`. It gets the wrist (the forearm's tail), the hand's direction, the wing-plane normal,
  the arm's length and the tip radius.
  - The last arm segment is digit 0's metacarpal. Other digits root at the wrist and fan
    backward through the module's angle.
  - The thumb (a `foot` on a wing) roots at the wrist too.
  - Digit bones are tubes, section `digit`, named `wing.L.d1.0…`. Body coordinates treat
    `digit` as `limb`, and digits are not hit capsules.
- **`WingRig`** extends `LimbChainRig` with:
  - `digits: number[][]`;
  - `normal: Vec3`, the wing plane in bind;
  - `span` and `area` (metres, m²) for 10.4;
  - `poses: Record<string, number[]>`, local quaternions over `bones`, then digits, then
    feather bones.
- Fins use `LimbChainRig` and no poses.

## The fold (decision 4)

The membrane module's `hooks.wing(params)` returns a style:

- `bind: { elbow, wrist }` (degrees);
- `fold: { sweep, droop, roll, elbow, wrist, digit, flex }`;
- `stack` and `cover` for insects.

With no membrane, a wing folds as a bat's.

1. **Shoulder**: a rotation in the root frame (its normal N, forward F, side S). It sweeps the
   humerus back by `sweep` about N, droops it by `droop` toward the flank, and rolls the wing
   plane by `roll` so the folded plane lies along the flank.
   - Bat: sweep 110, droop 35, roll 80.
   - Insect: sweep 160, droop 5, roll 0, a flat wing back over the abdomen; `tent` tilts broad
     wings 25° down outward.
   - Case: sweep 170, droop 0.
2. **Elbow, wrist, digit joints**: turns about the wing-plane normal by the style's angles.
   - Bat: elbow −150, wrist +140, digits close to within 6° of each other.
   - Each digit's later joints flex by `flex` in a Z, so the folded bundle is about a forearm
     long.
3. **Clearance**: in the folded pose, every joint and bone midpoint must clear by its radius
   plus `0.02 L`:
   - the body (a field of the skeleton's bones and masses at any radius, wings left out, the
     same at every quality);
   - the arms' bones;
   - the ground (y ≥ radius).

   Points within the pectoral sphere are exempt. If one fails, the shoulder's droop shrinks and
   its roll opens in 4° steps, up to 20 steps. Then a compile warning (`wing_clearance`) names
   the wing.
4. **Stacking (insects)**:
   - Hind wings sit lowest, forewings above them, cases on top. Each step up is the plate's
     thickness plus `0.004 L`, applied as a turn about S at the shoulder.
   - Of the left and right wings, the left lies above.
   - A wing covered by a case (the case's module provides `cover`; same side, attached in front)
     folds its distal half under the case by a turn at its last joint.

The folded pose is stored as `poses.folded` and in `BonesData.rest`, deterministic and pure.

## Membranes and spars

Membrane modules build in the bind pose with a new context call,
`ctx.membrane(sheet, { opacity, translucency, roughness, veins })`. Sheets are built by
helpers, so modules never handle weights:

- `ctx.panel(a, b, rows, cols, edge)`: between two spars;
- `ctx.plate(outline, frame, bone)`: rigid;
- `ctx.cards(...)`: feathers.

Spars come from `ctx.spars`, as polylines with their bones:

- `arm`;
- `digits[i]`;
- `flank`: the body's side from the wing root back to the nearest same-side leg's hip, or 0.6
  of the torso behind the root when there is none. It is snapped onto the skin by marching the
  SDF, as sockets are.
- `leg`: that leg from the hip to the knee, or none.

What each module builds:

- **`membrane.bat`**:
  - **panels between consecutive digits**, the wrist to the tips, the trailing edge dipping by
    `scallop` × the gap;
  - **the inner panel** between arm + last digit and flank + leg, or flank alone with
    `trailing: body` or no leg behind;
  - **a leading strip**, 12% of the forearm's length, from the shoulder to the wrist.
  - Opacity 1; translucency from the parameter.
- **`membrane.insect`**: a plate in the wing plane, from the outline for its `shape`, `width` ×
  length wide. It is rigid to the wing bone, with veins from the root. Opacity is
  `1 − 0.8 × translucency`.
- **`membrane.feather`**:
  - primaries fanned from the hand past the tip, `length` × the hand's length, `tips` shaping
    the last five (rounded, pointed, or fingered and spread);
  - secondaries along the forearm, pointing back;
  - two short covert rows.
  - Feather groups get bones (decision 6), and colour runs to `tipColor`.
- **`membrane.case`**: a domed shell (`dome`, `ridges`) built in the folded frame so that its
  medial edge lies on the midline when folded, then carried back to bind. It is chitin in
  `parts`, rigid to the case's bone, and its module provides `cover`.
- **`membrane.fin`**: `rays` rays fanned through `width` × 90° behind the fin limb, with panels
  between them (plain weights, since fins do not fold).
- **`fin.dorsal`, `fin.tail`**: row-built outlines (`shape`, `sweep`; `forked`, `crescent`,
  `rounded` upright, `flukes` flat) with interior vertices weighted to the bones they cover.

**Stations.**

- Every panel has stations at its spars' joints and at every 0.15 L between them, at most 10.
- Each station adds two bones (section `station`, skin-weighted only) named
  `<limb>.s<panel>.<k>.a` and `.b`, parented to the spar bones.
- A panel's vertex at station parameter (k + u), fraction f across, has weights
  `(1−u)(1−f)·a_k`, `(1−u)f·b_k`, `u(1−f)·a_{k+1}` and `u·f·b_{k+1}`.
- Panels between digits get stations too, so flight's in-plane flexing stays exact.

The membrane mesh carries positions, normals, indices, four skin weights per vertex, `color`,
`info` (opacity, translucency, roughness), `vein` (strength, coordinate) and `body`
(spine, height, span, across), so the pattern stack can run on it.

Its triangles are capped at 12k at medium; rows, stations and feather counts scale down to fit.

## Material

`membraneMaterial()` in `packages/three`:

- double-sided, `forceSinglePass`, with normals flipped toward the eye;
- the base colour and the pattern layers of region `wings` through the shared kit, then veins
  as anti-aliased lines;
- back-lighting `translucency × colour × max(0, −N·L)`, warm-tinted;
- roughness per vertex.

Shadows are cast as opaque, a known simplification. The export writes membranes as their own
primitive (double-sided, vertex colours, `alphaMode: BLEND` when see-through). Its notes say
that translucency and veins are approximated in the vertex colours, which alias on small wings.

## Motion

- **The wings goal.** The controller blends each wing bone's local rotation from rest (folded)
  toward bind (spread) by `spread`.
  - `spread` is damped toward the goal over about 0.4 s × timeScale, every step after the body
    and before springs. It runs for legless bodies too, which today return early.
  - At rest the shoulders lift 2° with `breath`.
  - `roar` spreads to 0.8.
- **Feather groups** blend with their wing. **Stations** are posed last (`applyStations`), as
  helpers are.
- **Fins** keep their rest pose.
- **`applyRest(pose, rig, { jaw, blink, spread })`** replaces `applyFace` (kept as an alias).
  The render page always builds a pose with it, so plain renders show the rest pose. `render
  --pose spread` (CLI and MCP) spreads the wings and frames `spreadBounds`.

## Analysis

- **`wing_intersection`**, in the walk check:
  - Wing, digit and fin bones, and every 8th membrane vertex skinned by the pose, are tested
    against the body's capsules, the legs, the arms and the ground, frame by frame.
  - Skipped are vertices within 10% of a panel's body or leg spar, and the humerus inside the
    pectoral sphere.
  - The walk check also runs at spread 0.5 and 1 standing still, so `roar` and intermediate
    poses are covered.
  - The deepest pass over 1 cm warns, naming the wing, with fixes: attach higher or further
    forward, a shorter `length` or `span`, `trailing: "body"`.
- **`wing_clearance`** (compile) when the fold could not clear.
- **`measurements.wingspan`**. The description names the membrane, e.g. "leathery wings".

## Edge cases the fuzz will hit

- Any membrane module works on any wing or fin chain: rays fan on a wing, feathers line a fin.
- A `foot` on a wing grows its thumb at the wrist.
- Wings may hang from the neck or the tail. The fold uses the root's frame, so they work.
- A centre wing (side `center`) folds straight back with no roll.
- Legless winged bodies fold and spread.
- Panels with no flank partner (wings off the torso) skip the inner panel.

## Body plans and examples

- **`wyvern`**: `membrane.bat`, 4 fingers. It uses `trailing: "body"` as a walker; cave-bat
  shows `leg`.
- **`fish`**:
  - `fin.tail` (forked) and `fin.dorsal`;
  - pectoral fins (`at` 0.2, `angle` 120, `length` 0.3) and pelvic fins (`at` 0.55, `length`
    0.18);
  - media water;
  - it rests on its belly until 10.3. This changes the fish preset, and blueprints that extend
    it gain its fins: the changelog says so.
- **Examples:**
  - `ash-dragon`: quadruped, wings at `angle` 35;
  - `cave-bat`: wyvern, small, 5 fingers, big ears, trailing `leg`;
  - `storm-wyvern`;
  - `rhino-beetle`: hexapod with cases and insect wings;
  - `luna-moth`: broad insect wings in fore and hind pairs, with `wings`-region eye spots;
  - `reef-shark`: fish;
  - `griffin`: feathered wings, a beak and talons.
  - Each gets its render and golden, and is checked folded and spread, walking (the shark
    slithers) and within budgets.

## Budgets

- Draw calls: 4 with membranes, 5 with fur.
- Hard parts ≤ 20k triangles and membranes ≤ 12k at medium.
- Compile time ≤ 500 ms per example at medium. The fold samples the body field a few hundred
  times; stations and membranes are a few thousand vertices.
- Motion per creature ≤ 0.1 ms walking. Wings add one slerp per wing and feather bone, and
  stations two aims each; a dragon adds about 150 bones.
- `scripts/budgets.ts` counts the membrane draw call and triangles. `fingerprint` hashes
  membranes and `rest` when present, and `compiledTransferables` lists them.

## Tests

- **Unchanged:** every golden and render baseline without wings or fins.
- **Skeleton:**
  - wing bones are tubes and leave the grid's cell size unchanged;
  - digits root at the wrist;
  - the fold is deterministic and the same at every quality.
- **Fold:**
  - folded bones and membrane vertices outside the attachment band clear the body, arms and
    ground by their radius;
  - the folded span is under 40% of the spread one, and no digit tip lies further back than the
    rump;
  - insect wings stack in order under their cases;
  - a case's medial edge lies within 1 cm of the midline.
- **Ruled-surface oracle (stations):** across all panels, the largest distance of a skinned
  membrane vertex from `lerp(A'(s), B'(s), f)` stays under 1% of the panel's width, with no
  flipped triangles. It is checked at rest, at spread 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1, under a ±45°
  shoulder stroke, and with a leg swung 60°.
- **Feathers:** folded cards point back (−forward) within 35° and never into the body; cards in
  different ranks keep their offset.
- **Rest and export:**
  - `Pose` starts folded;
  - `createCreatureObject`'s bones default to rest;
  - a glb of a winged example round-trips with folded node defaults and clips that keep the
    wing folded.
- **Motion:**
  - `wings` spreads over about 0.4 s;
  - `roar` spreads;
  - walking keeps the wings folded;
  - no `wing_intersection` on any example.
- **Render:** `--pose spread` frames the wingspan; labels follow the wings; visual regression
  for each new example.
- **Harness:** every built membrane module and fin part, with its defaults, its example, budgets
  and the same twice. **Fuzz:** the combinations above compile.

## Not in 9.3

- Flight, gliding and hovering (10.4); swimming and fin beats (10.3).
- Body feathers, and fur on wings.
- A bat preset.
- Torn membranes.
- A glTF translucency extension.

## As built

What changed from the design while building it, and why. The tests, examples and budgets hold
the rest as written.

| Area | Designed | Built | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Covered wings | Distal half turned under the case at the last joint | Folded on edge along the back, doubled back at the last joint like a turned page (a half turn about the crease, so the membrane still hangs below), hanging inside the body under the case | Flat, a plate wider than the abdomen crossed it and showed at the sides; an in-plane half turn flips the membrane above the arm |
| Wing cases | Stacked on top of the wings | `shell` style: no lifting clear, no stacking, the pair meets at the midline; the case's arm bones draw nothing | Stacked and lifted, the cases stood up off the back |
| Fold plane | `lie` 0–90 | A negative `lie` turns the plane toward the flank, for modules that want it; feathered wings keep 0 and lay each feather against the body instead | Feathers in a plane tangent at a root high on the back flared out like a skirt |
| Feathers | Groups of 3 or 4 cards per bone | One card per bone, each folded tangent to the body where it roots, shaft back, upper face out | Rigid groups kept their spread fan when folded, so feathers stuck out; per-card bones lie in clean rows (the griffin adds about 100 bones and stays inside the motion budget) |
| Stations | At joints and every 0.15 L, at most 10; roll from the spars' central difference | At most 10 (the code had 12); each station's roll carried on from the one before | The central difference nearly cancels where a spar folds back on itself and flipped the roll, throwing vertices up to two thirds of the panel off the surface |
| Ruled-surface oracle | Under 1% at every spread | Under 0.1% spread, under 2% at spread 0.75 and under a ±45° stroke with a leg swung 60°, under 15% folded or partly folded; leading strips left out | Folding, the inner panel twists through itself at the elbow (its aim turns about 140° between neighbouring stations) and four rigid stations cannot follow it; it is hidden in the folded bundle, and renders at spread 0.3 and 0.5 show no tearing. Leading strips ride offsets of the arm's own bones, so they have no second spar |
| Folded span | Under 40% of the spread span; no digit tip behind the rump | Leathery wings fold to about 55%, their tips trailing along the tail within a torso length of the rump; feathered wings 32% | A Z or a curl in the fingers either splayed the membrane into wide triangles or rolled the wing out from the body; the long-fingered fold reads as a cloak |
| Wing roots | At 0.85 of the section's radius; a sphere of 1.3 × the root radius | 1.1 root radii off the skin; a tapered capsule from 1.6 root radii inside the skin to the joint, blended wide; fins have none | The sphere read as a ball on the shoulder; fins grow flush |
| Fins | Flat limb only without a membrane | Fin limbs are always flat (1.3 × 0.35 before a membrane, at half the radius); `membrane.fin` stops its last ray short of pointing back along the body | Round, full-width rays looked like stubby arms; rays past 90° turned into the flank |
| Membrane `body` attribute | Spine, height, span, across per vertex | Derived in the shader: `spine` from the creature's rest bounds, `height` from the normal, `limb` from the spar coordinate | The same patterns, with no extra attribute |
| Wing tubes in the skin | `Surface.wings` 1 on wing tubes | Encoded as `limb + 2` in the body coordinates, decoded by the surface, the CPU bake and fur | The region vector has four channels |
| `wing_intersection` | Vertices within 10% of a body or leg spar skipped | Also those riding mostly on a wing's or fin's root bone; warns past max(1 cm, 1% of the torso); a wing covered by a case is exempt until fully spread | Fin roots sit in the body by design; covered wings rest inside it |
| Motion cost | One slerp per bone, two aims per station | The controller solves stations only in `applyStations` (other solves skip them) and only the wing bones after `applyWings`; `applyStations` is allocation-free | The cave bat spent 0.15 ms a frame, over the 0.1 ms budget; now at most 0.09 |
| Labels | Wing markers and sockets | Also part markers of sheets and shells ride their bone; pieces built in model space mark their middle | The membrane labels hung where the spread wing would be, and the beak's at the origin |
| Determinism | | The nearest bone at a vertex is the first within 1e-9 | Two bones tie at a toe joint; Node and Chrome's trigonometry differed in the last bit and picked different bones (only joint indices change, so only bog-troll's golden moved) |

`setWings(spread)` on the controller and on the runtime's `Creature` holds a spread when no
action asks otherwise, so games can open the wings before flight (10.4) arrives. Budgets,
measured with `pnpm budgets`: every example compiles under 500 ms in Node, membranes are under
2k triangles (the 12k cap was never needed, so nothing scales down to it), winged examples are 4
draw calls and 5 with fur, and motion is at most 0.09 ms a creature.
