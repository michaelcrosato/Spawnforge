# Design: root motion in actions; jump and pounce (milestone 10.2)

Plan 1's actions move the body and head about a planted root: a bite lunges, a roar rears.
10.2 lets an action carry the whole creature through the air. The controller owns the physics,
and the action module only says how to leap. So one `jump` works on a wolf, a horse or a raptor
on any ground the game gives.

## What an action module gives

A new `leap(params)` hook returns:

- `crouch` and `recover`: seconds, for a creature with 1 m hips (scaled by √hip as every action
  is), spent gathering before takeoff and absorbing the landing.
- `angle`: the launch angle above horizontal.
- `reach` and `most`: how far it leaps with no target, and at most, in hip heights. So a
  wolf's jump is wolf-sized.
- `height`: the least height the arc clears in its middle (`jump`'s `height` parameter, for
  leaping over an obstacle).
- `head`: lands so the head, not the feet, meets the target (`pounce`).

Its `goals` hook gets `ctx.leap = { takeoff, land }` as shares of progress, so it can crouch
before takeoff, tuck and look in the air, and absorb after landing. Its `events` hook gets the
same, so `pounce` puts `bite-contact` at the landing. The controller adds `takeoff` and `land`
events to every leap.

## Planning the arc

On the action's first step, when the game's ground is known:

1. **Where it lands.** The target projected on the ground, or straight ahead by `reach`. A
   pounce lands short by how far the head sits ahead of the root, plus a little, so the snout
   meets the prey. The distance is kept within `most`.
2. **The arc.** For a horizontal distance `d` and a height change `Δh`, the launch speed at
   angle θ is `v² = g·d² / (2·cos²θ·(d·tanθ − Δh))`. Starting from the module's angle, the
   controller takes the lowest angle (in 2.5° steps up to 80°) whose arc stays above the ground
   at 15 points: 2% of hip height near the ends, and `height` (at least 5% of hip height) over
   the middle half. Rough ground and a hurdle therefore steepen the leap rather than clip it.
3. **Timing.** The action's length becomes crouch + flight + recovery, and the events are
   placed on that.

## Flying the arc

- **Crouch.** The creature stands and turns to face where it lands. Its legs step as for
  turning on the spot, and the body follows the module's crouch goal.
- **Takeoff.** Every foot leaves the ground (`feet()` reports them all unplanted). The root
  follows the parabola exactly, horizontally from start to landing point and vertically
  `y₀ + v_y·t − g·t²/2`. The body pitches nose up as it leaves and nose down as it comes in.
  The legs hang below the hips and tuck by up to a quarter of hip height mid-flight.
- **Land.** The root is put exactly at the planned landing point, on the ground there. Every
  foot plants under its hip, toes rolled onto the ground, so the landing error is the plan's,
  which is none on reachable targets. The rest of the action (the absorb, a pounce's bite) runs
  on planted feet, and then the creature carries on as before.

## Clips and export

- **Root motion.** `bakeClips` bakes a leaping action unaimed (as far as it leaps by itself)
  and keeps its root track. The root starts at the origin and travels with the leap, and the
  clip says `rootMotion: true`. Every other clip stays in place, as before.
- **Export.** The root's position track is in the `.glb`, and the clip's extras say
  `rootMotion`. runtime.md says how to apply the track or strip it.

## Done when

- Jumps to a target land within 5% of the aimed distance on flat and rough ground, without the
  body going into the ground.
- The clips keep their root track.

`jumps.test.ts` checks both, and also that a pounce bites as it lands with its head at the
target, and that a hurdle's height is cleared.
