# Design: heads, mouths, teeth, eyes and beaks (milestone 8.3)

Plan 1's head is one rounded cone with a jaw cone below it, meshed on the body's grid. A mouth
is a cut along the mouth line, the pieces below it follow the jaw, and a dark ellipsoid pouch sits
inside. Teeth are cones at the mouth line, sized in torso lengths. Eyes are glossy balls that blink
by squashing. Blind reviewers in every plan 1 gate named the faults: mouths are not visibly cut,
teeth look glued on and sparse, and eyes are big and glossy. 8.3 rebuilds the head.

The plan names one max step: how eyelids and lips are built, as pieces of the skin mesh or as
separate skinned pieces on bones of their own. 9.1's several heads build on that choice. The
question rests on another one the plan does not settle, so this step decides that too: how a head
gets enough resolution for any detail at all.

## Today

- **Resolution.** Every example meshes on a grid of 96 cells along its longest side at medium.
  On the grey wolf a cell is 2.1 cm, and its head is about 12 cells long and 8 wide. On the reed
  viper a cell is 2.2 cm and the head is three cells wide, so a brow, nostrils or a lip cannot show
  on the grid at all. `plan.md` makes detail under two cells a shader matter.
- **The cut** (`mouth.ts`, `cutMouth`) sorts head triangles in front of the mouth corner into
  upper and lower by their centroid, then duplicates the shared vertices. So the cut is a
  staircase along the grid. Every vertex not below the cut loses its jaw weight, including the
  cheek behind the corner, so an open mouth tears at the corner.
- **Inside** (`innerMouth`) is a closed ellipsoid with inward normals. Its lower half is on the
  jaw, and it is flagged `body.z = -1`; the skin shader paints that flag flat dark red.
- **Teeth** (`teeth.row`) are 10 cones a side by default, set just inside `mouthPoint`. Their
  lengths are torso lengths, so a long serpent's fangs come out as planks.
- **Eyes** (`eye.basic`) have a radius of `size` × L, roughness 0.08, and are squashed along
  their bone for a blink. Live, `pose-sync.ts` does it; exported, `export.ts` writes scale
  tracks.
- **In the format.** `body.head.lips`, `tongue` and `brow` validate and are reported `not_built`.
  `beak` is a stub (`planned: '8.3'`).

## The max step: lips and lids are skin; the head gets its own resolution

What follows is as built, after the review (below) and renders of every example.

### Head resolution: refine the head after meshing

After surface nets and skin weights, each head with its jaw is refined in place (`head.ts`,
`refineHeads`, over `refine.ts`). Edges in the head region are split until none is longer than
the head's target edge length. Each new vertex steps onto the creature's field with its details
in it: the grid sampled the field without them (`SdfEvaluator.details = false`), since anything
under a cell would only alias there.

- **The region** is every triangle whose three vertices carry at least half their weight on the
  head instance's own bones (`head`, `jaw`).
- **The target edge** starts at `r0 / k` (k is 8 at low, 12 at medium and 16 at high), where r0
  is the skull's radius, and is never finer than a sixteenth of a cell.
- **The allowance.** The heads share 1,500, 4,000 or 9,000 triangles. The edge also grows, by 8%
  steps, until the whole skin fits under 97% of 10k, 30k or 66k. That count includes the body
  (whose surface-nets budget stays 27k), the swept tubes, the lids and an estimate of the mouth's
  inside. A head with no room left is not refined.
- **Splitting** works on edges, so it never leaves cracks. Each pass:
  - gives every edge longer than `1.4 e` a midpoint, which both its triangles share;
  - re-triangulates every triangle with split edges: two, three or four triangles, keeping the
    winding.

  Only triangles touching the region can gain a split, so the rest of the body is set aside.
  Edges are handled in index order, so the result is the same every time.
- **Details** get edges at most one halving finer: within reach of a brow, cheekbone or nostril,
  the target is `max(finest, ½ detail radius, ½ e)`. The lips run the length of the mouth and are
  not refined finer.
- **Projection**: one Newton step against the primitives within reach of the point (a handful,
  not the block's 40–50). The normal is the field's gradient.
- **Relaxation**, once at the end: every region vertex whose triangles are all in the region
  moves halfway to its neighbours' centroid within its tangent plane, then steps back onto the
  surface.
- **Weights** of a new vertex are its edge's two ends averaged, keeping the top slots.
- **Stopping**: no long edges left, or five passes.

The cost at medium is +8 to +20 ms (warm minimum) on the examples near the 500 ms budget, whose
bulky bodies leave their heads little room, and about +50 ms on the wolf and the ridgeback
(about 250 ms). Refining the head, rather than meshing it on a grid of its own, keeps one
closed, connected skin with no seam.

### Lips: pieces of the skin mesh

- **The ridge.** Lips are detail masses on the head chain, six short cones per side along the
  mouth's outline: the upper lip's on the head bone, the lower lip's on the jaw. The outline
  (`mouthShape`) comes from rays in the cut from the midpoint between the corners, marched
  through the head and jaw's own field without details, then resampled at even arc lengths. The
  ridge's radius is `lips × 0.18 × r`, tapering toward the corners, set in from the outline by
  0.6 of itself. It shows from about `lips: 0.5`.
- **The lip line**, at any `lips`, is a crease: outer skin within half a lip's thickness of the
  cut carries `crease` up to 0.85, which the skin shader already darkens. A shut mouth shows where
  it opens.
- **An exact cut** (`cutMouth`). In front of the mouth corner, for the head region's vertices,
  `c(v) = min(y − lineY(z), d_jaw − d_head)` is evaluated:
  - the first term is the mouth line;
  - the second sends skin made by the jaw's own shape (a chin that reaches past the snout, as on
    the viper) to the jaw even above the line.

  Every edge whose ends have opposite signs gets a vertex at `c = 0`, shared by both triangles.
  Triangles split by it, so none straddles the cut. A vertex that both sides of the mouth use is
  duplicated for the lower side. One a triangle outside the mouth touches is not: the opening
  ends there, at the corner. The cut's edge is the longest chain of boundary edges, walked from
  one end.
- **Weights.** Below the cut: the jaw. Above it: no jaw (only the head, where the jaw was all a
  vertex had). Behind the corner the cheek and throat blend toward the jaw by
  `(1 − smoothstep(0, 0.6 r, behind)) × smoothstep(0, 0.3 r, below)`. So the opening tapers into
  the corner, and the throat follows the jaw down instead of tearing.
- **A jaw thinner than a grid cell** stays in the field with its head. As a swept tube it used to
  hang inside the head and show when the mouth opened.

### The inside of the mouth

The inside (`mouthInside`) is lofted from the cut's edge, so with the skin it is closed: every
edge at the head has two triangles.

- **The strip.** Every boundary vertex is the first ring of its side, so the skin runs on into the
  mouth. Each further ring point (`stripPoint`) moves toward the midline at the same distance
  along the head, and toward the throat near the tip, by at most 45% of the way:
  - ring 1, the lip's inner edge: by the lip thickness `T = (0.04 + 0.2 × lips) × r`, lifted
    `0.02 T`;
  - ring 2, the gums, where teeth stand: by `1.3 T`, lifted `0.8 T`;
  - ring 3: by `1.8 T`, lifted `1.2 T`.

  Every lift, and every deeper height, is at most 0.6 of the room above (or below) the point
  inside the head and jaw alone.
- **Palate and floor.** Three more rings run from the gums toward the midpoint between the
  corners, rising to 0.6 of the room there, and close on a centre point at the throat. Deeper in,
  every third column is enough; the cut's edge zips onto the first ring.
- **Corner walls** join the upper and lower columns at each end and stretch as the jaw opens.
  The corner's shared vertex makes no triangle with itself.
- **The tongue** (`tongue`: `flat`, the default, or `forked`):
  - a flattened tube on the floor, all on the jaw, from just behind the corners to 80% of the
    way to the tip (`forked`: 88%);
  - about half the corners' width (`forked`: narrower, splitting into two prongs over its last
    fifth);
  - `none` builds nothing.
- **Shading.** Inside vertices carry their depth and kind:
  - `body.z = -1 - depth`, with depth running from 0 at the lips to 1 at the throat;
  - `body.w` is the kind: 0 cavity, 1 lips and gums, 2 tongue.

  `shadeMouth(kit, limb, crease)` in core gives the colours, so the TSL skin material and the
  vertex-colour bake share one formula. The cavity goes from wet red to near black, gums are
  pink, and the tongue is a lighter pink that darkens with depth. Roughness is 0.3, with no skin
  relief inside.

**Why not separate lip pieces on bones of their own:** a separate tube along the mouth would sit
on the skin with a visible seam. It would also need the skin's pattern colours copied to it, and
would add bones to every jaw for no motion the jaw does not already give.

### Eyelids: skin pieces on lid bones

- **Two lids per eye** (`lids.ts`), upper and lower, each a shell around the eye's centre, drawn
  with the skin. Its profile runs from 1.01 × the eye's radius on the inside of the rim to 1.08 ×
  outside, so the rim has thickness. Its normals lean toward the opening there.
- **Shape.** In the lids' frame (side X, up Y, out Z), each lid covers 130° past its edge, pole to
  pole. Every edge is a great circle through the two side poles. Seen from the front that makes
  an almond-shaped opening, and rotating a lid about X keeps it one. At rest the upper edge is at
  `φu = 38° − 30° × squint` and the lower at `φl = −34°`.
- **The frame** is 70% the socket's normal and 30% the eye's look, so both corners of the opening
  sit at the skin's depth (the review's point).
- **Bones.**
  - Each lid gets a bone (`eye.<part id>.upper`, `.lower`, section `lid`, not skinned to the
    field).
  - It is parented to the bone the eye hangs from, never to the eye's own bone. So the ball turns
    inside lids that stay put, and with one centre it can never poke through.
  - The bone runs along `out` with `up` up, so its local X is the frame's −side.
- **The blink** is a driven chain:
  `{ owner: <eye part id>, bones: [upper, lower], drive: 'blink', poses: { closed: [a, b] } }`.
  - Each angle is `meet − edge`, turned about the bone's local X. The lids meet at
    `φl + 0.25 (φu − φl)`, so the upper lid does most of the travel.
  - `applyFace` turns every blink chain by `blink × angle`, for the controller and for the render
    page alike. No module's name is in the core.
- **Colour.** Lid vertices copy the body coordinates of the nearest skin vertex on the head. A
  `volumeOf` leaves them out of the body's closed surface.
- **Pose sync and export.** Nothing squashes the eyes any more. Baked clips record every bone's
  rotation, so idle clips carry the lids' tracks and an exported blink uses them. Eyes without
  lids (`lids: false`) do not blink: snakes, fish and insects.
- **Who builds them.** The eye pipeline is the core's (`EmitOptions.eye`): `eye.basic` asks for
  lids with `lids` and `squint`. The general `bones` hook from the rig sketch waits for 9.4.

**Why not lids carved from the skin mesh:** the skin around an eye has no spare surface to cover
the eye with. Closing a lid would stretch a ring of skin across the whole ball. Skinning it to a
lid bone would drag the brow along, and it could not export as a clean lid rotation.

### What 9.1 builds on

Each piece already works per head instance:

- per head with a jaw: the mouth shape, the head's details on its chain, refinement of its
  region (the heads share the allowance), the cut, the inside and the corner weights;
- per eye: the lids, on the head the eye hangs from.

With several heads, the loops run more than once, and bone names follow 7.4's rule.

## The skull: brow, cheekbones and nostrils

Detail masses and carves on the head chain, all at the head's scale:

- **Brow** (`brow` 0–1, default 0.2): over each eye part on this head, a cone from 30° inside the
  eye's angle to 10° outside it, at the eye's `at` plus 0.03. Its radius is
  `brow × 0.4 × r(at)`, half of it showing. The core finds eye parts by their module's material
  `eye`, not by type. A head with no eyes gets one ridge across the forehead.
- **Cheekbones**: on any head with a jaw, a cone on each side, radius 0.1 r0, from the eye line
  back toward the hinge (`at` 0.8, angles 95° to 100°). They are never longer than 1.5 r0, so a
  long snout does not grow a ridge along its whole length.
- **Nostrils**: two carves, the field's one subtraction (kind 3), on the top front of the snout
  (`at` 0.05, angle ±32°). Each is 0.24 r1 across and 1.6 times as long, blended by half its
  radius.
  - A carve applies inside its own chain, after the chain's masses, with a smooth maximum, so it
    never cuts another chain.
  - Culling counts its bounds and blend like a mass, and the nearest-primitive bookkeeping
    ignores it.

## Teeth: `teeth.row`

| Parameter | Before | 8.3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `scale` | (new) | 0.2–3, default 1: tooth size relative to the head |
| `fangScale` | (new) | 0.2–3, default 1: fang size relative to the head |
| `spacing` | (new) | 0–2, default 0.15: the gap between teeth as a share of a tooth's width |
| `incisors` | (new) | 0–4, default 2: small front teeth before the fangs, when `count` is not written |
| `count` | default 10 per side | optional; written, it places that many as plan 1 did |
| `length`, `fangLength` | defaults 0.025 L, 0.06 L | optional; written, still torso lengths, and they win |

- **Sockets.** `ctx.mouth(t, row, side)` is now a point on the gums (ring 2). `t` runs by length
  along them. The normal points into the opening, and the radius is the skull's: sizes follow
  the head, not the narrowing snout.
- **Lengths**, as shares of the skull's radius:
  - incisors 0.1;
  - fangs 0.45 × `fangScale` (lower ones 0.7 of that);
  - cheek teeth from 0.16 down to 0.1 toward the corner, × `scale`.

  Bases are 0.3, 0.2 and 0.35 of the length. A quarter of each tooth stands in the gum.
- **Lean.** Each tooth leans out of the mouth over its length: fangs by 0.3 of it, so a wolf's
  upper fangs show over the lower lip when the mouth is shut, as a dog's do.
- **Density.** Without `count`, teeth are packed by width and `spacing` from the tip to 95% of
  the row: incisors, then fangs, then cheek teeth. That is about 20 a side on the wolf. A packed
  row jitters each length by ±8% from the part's own stream; a written `count` keeps plan 1's
  exact lengths.
- **Stats.** The row reports its largest tooth and teeth per row (`ctx.measure`), and stats read
  those before any parameter.

## Eyes: `eye.basic`

| Parameter | Before | 8.3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `scale` | (new) | 0.2–3, default 1: radius `0.18 × scale × r`, where r is the radius of what it sits on |
| `size` | default 0.022 L | optional; written, still torso lengths, and it wins |
| `lids` | (new) | default true: eyelids that blink |
| `squint` | (new) | 0–1, default 0.15: how far the upper lid hangs at rest |

- **Gloss.** Roughness goes from 0.12 to 0.24 (`EYE_ROUGHNESS`, shared by the shader and the
  bake).
- **Presets and themes.** They use `scale`: the serpent's eyes have no lids, and the serpent also
  has a forked tongue and `lips: 0`. Hexapod, octopod, fish and the insect theme have no lids.

## Beaks: `beak`

- **Built from mouth positions.** Each half is a closed tube of crescent cross-sections, built
  from `ctx.around(t, row, angle)`: the skin around the snout at mouth position `t`, from the
  left lip line (0°) over the top (or the chin) to the right (180°).
  - The outer arc stands proud of the skin by `0.12 r × depth`; the inner arc lies just inside.
  - Rings run from `t` 0.85 to 0.09, not to the tip, where the two sides meet.
- **Past the snout**, six more rings run on by `0.6 × length × snout`:
  - `hooked` curls down, and its lower half ends under the hook (0.4 of the reach);
  - `straight` tapers to a point;
  - `broad` widens and flattens.
- **Bones.** The upper half moves with the head and the lower with the jaw, so the beak opens
  with the mouth. A beak and teeth together validate.
- **The example.** `terror-bird` is a new example: a biped with a hooked beak, `lips: 0`, a
  heavy brow, squinting eyes and talons.

## Format and docs

- **Defaults.** No value changes meaning. The new parameters have defaults. `size`, `length`,
  `fangLength` and `count` lose theirs: written values keep their meaning, and unwritten ones
  take the new defaults.
- **The judged blueprints.** The examples, presets and themes move to the relative sizes. The
  phase 4 run's saved blueprints keep what they wrote, so gate 8 sees their new mouths, lids,
  skull and gloss but their old eye and tooth sizes.
- **Not built.** `notBuilt` no longer lists `lips`, `tongue` and `brow`, and `beak` is no longer a
  stub.
- **Generated files and docs.** The catalogue and the JSON Schema are regenerated.
  `blueprint.md` has the head section and recipes, and `architecture.md`, `runtime.md` and
  `rig.md` follow the build.

## Checks

- **Refinement** (`core/compile/refine.test.ts`), on an icosahedron projected onto a sphere:
  - every long edge is split;
  - the mesh stays closed and on the surface;
  - no triangle faces inward;
  - limits are finer where asked, the rest is left alone, and it is the same every time.
- **Heads** (`modules/heads.test.ts`), on the wolf, viper, ridgeback and terror bird:
  - the mouth's inside closes the skin, with no open or tangled edge at the head;
  - the cut's vertices come in pairs, one on the head and its copy on the jaw;
  - with the jaw open, the tongue moves and the palate stays;
  - `tongue: none` builds none;
  - the lids' edges are apart at rest and meet at blink 1;
  - the idle clip turns the lids;
  - teeth pack 12–30 a side by default and keep a written count and length exactly, and stats
    read them;
  - a written eye size is exact and `scale` is relative;
  - the lids leave the mass alone.
- **Export** (`three/export.test.ts`): the exported idle clip has the four lids' rotation tracks
  and no scale tracks.
- **Updated tests:**
  - the rig-lists test now expects the blink chains;
  - the anatomy test counts anatomy's masses apart from head details;
  - the muscle-0 golden is re-pinned (with `UPDATE_GOLDEN=1`), since the heads changed for every
    muscle value.
- **The harness** builds `beak` like every other part. The corpus, goldens and render baselines
  are re-recorded.
- **Budgets.** Every example's skin is ≤ 30k triangles at medium and compiles within budget; the
  fuzz runs 1,000 clean with a median of 353 ms (≤ 400).

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| A finer grid everywhere | Triangles grow with the square of the resolution; the body does not need it |
| A second grid for the head, stitched to the body | Stitching two surface-nets meshes needs an octree-style crack fix at the border; refinement needs none |
| Head detail only in the shader (normal maps) | Silhouettes stay blobs; the plan asks for SDF details; export bakes colour only |
| Lips as separate pieces on lip bones | A visible seam and copied colours, plus bones with no motion of their own (above) |
| Lids carved from the skin | No spare skin to close; the brow stretches; no clean lid track (above) |
| Lids in the eye mesh with a skin colour | The eye material and a flat colour show a seam where the lid meets patterned skin |
| A `bones` hook on `eye.basic` now | The core already builds the eye's bone and mesh; the hook earns its place with 9.4's chains |
| Nostrils painted on | The plan asks for SDF details; a carve reads in silhouette and in shading |
| Teeth sized by the torso, defaults smaller | A long serpent still gets planks; size has to follow the head |

**Found while prototyping, before the review came back:**

- A jaw thinner than a grid cell was swept as a separate tube inside the head; with the mouth
  open it hung there as a lump (the viper's). A jaw is now in the field whenever its head is.
- Skin that the jaw's own shape makes above the mouth line (a chin reaching past the snout)
  belongs to the jaw: the cut takes the lower of the line and `distance to jaw − distance to
  head`.
- Upper vertices in the mouth whose only weight was the jaw now follow the head.
- `applyFace(pose, rig, jaw, blink)` in core opens jaws and closes lids for the controller and
  for `render --jaw --blink`, which shows contact sheets with the mouth open.

**Verdict.** The reviewer agreed with lids as separate shells on their own bones, drawn in the
skin. It agreed with refinement over a second, stitched grid, provided it is cheap and adaptive,
which it now is. On the lips it suggested dropping the SDF ridge and getting thickness from the
strip's roll and a darker line. The ridge stays for blueprints that ask for full lips (it shows
from about `lips: 0.5` with adaptive refinement). The default look comes from the roll, and a
darkened lip line comes from the crease coordinate along the cut.
